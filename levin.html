CRR and Michael Levin's research programme Draft

CRR, Levin and the Origin of Forms

Michael Levin's laboratory treats the growth and repair of a body as problem-solving: cells working towards a target shape, remembering it, and losing that grip as they age. It publishes its computer models. In October 2026 CRR was used as a source of questions about two of them, in four studies run on the authors' own code.

For each study the page shows three things: what CRR suggested, what was actually found, and what CRR enabled, with the grade the record gives. The short version: CRR's own operations earned no credit anywhere. But the questions it posed led to real results about the lab's models. The clearest is in ageing. A search guided by CRR found a repair rule that keeps every organ of the model intact 99–100% of the time, as well as the model manages with no noise at all. The rule itself is ordinary.

Ageing in the lab's own model

The model is Pio-Lopez, Hartl and Levin's ageing simulation (Advanced Science, 2025): a 16 × 16 grid of cells, each running a small evolved network, that grows a smiley face and then slowly loses it under noise. Fitness counts correctly typed cells, at most 256. The paper reads ageing as "a loss of goal-directedness", and shows that injecting a young cell state into damaged cells rejuvenates the face. Three frozen studies asked what CRR's account of time says about this.

1. When and from what to repair

CRR suggested

Repair at CRR "cuts", when accumulated cell-type change reaches a fixed size. Rebuild from a seed that weights the organ's recent past.

Found

Neither operation helped. Cut timing equalled a plain clock; the past-weighted seed was worse than the paper's reset. But the search found two ordinary improvements: repair early and often (+10.8), and write the mature organ state rather than the primordial one (+3.0). Together they hold every organ intact 99–100% of the time.

CRR enabled

The design question, posing timing and content as one axis, which surfaced both improvements. Grades: DISTINCTION as heuristic; NOTHING for the cut and the seed; mildly MISLED for recency weighting.

Read the chart from the top. The paper's rule (repair when an organ has partly failed, to the primordial state) lifts fitness from 196.3 to 211.6. Repairing at CRR cuts reaches 222.4, but a clock at the same rate reaches 222.1, and repairing at the first wrong cell reaches 223.2 with fewer writes. Swapping the content to the canonical mature organ state brings the best rules to 224.8–226.5, the level the evolved controller holds with no noise at all (226). Frequent repair to the target cancels the organ damage completely in this model.

Why the cut adds nothing. Event-triggered sampling beats a clock when the event is defined on the error being controlled (Åström & Bernhardsson 2002). A wrong cell is the error; CRR's cut counts cell-type activity, much of it harmless fluctuation, so under steady noise it behaves like a clock. Why recent weighting hurts. The organ's recent past is already drifting; the useful information is in the oldest record (the genome-coded primordial state) or the canonical target. The paper's own repair is the q = 1 end of CRR's regeneration family, and the data favour that end.

2. Does the capacity to repair decline with age?

CRR suggested

Read a life as ruptures and regenerations. Separate the capacity to regenerate from the chance of a rupture that cannot be regenerated. Treat each rupture as a first passage. And: CRR's coherence might give early warning.

Found

Repair capacity does not decline with age, and there is no senescence: the hazard of losing an organ does not rise. An eye is lost when noise first creates a lesion of one or two cells it cannot absorb. Losses do not cascade. Repairing at the first wrong cell is best. CRR's coherence gave no early warning.

CRR enabled

The questions. Grades: DISTINCTION (capacity against hazard); QUESTION (cut as first passage, which led to the critical-lesion experiments); NOTHING for coherence as a warning signal.

In this model, then, "loss of goal-directedness" is not a decline in what the cells can do. Eyes are fragile attractors inside a robust face, and ageing is noise waiting to create the first lesion an eye cannot absorb. The practical rule follows: intervene before the first lesion grows. The paper's trigger fires at four or more wrong cells per eye and gives up about 8–10 fitness units.

Look closer: the five answers and the frozen predictions
  • Repair capacity by age (Q2): eye-centre cell 61/63 young against 34/36 mature (p = 0.62); cheek patch of 3 cells 200/200 at every age; eye patch of 3 cells 0/60 against 0/35.
  • Senescence (Q1): across 200 unrepaired lifetimes of 2,000 steps, the hazard changes by −0.06 per 1,000 steps [−0.28, +0.17].
  • Critical lesion (Q2b, Q2c): 14/29, 23/26, 24/25 and 22/22 eyes lost for 1–4 cells; every single eye cell is a potential trigger, 8–77% by position, lower edge highest.
  • Cascade (Q3): −0.01 [−0.21, +0.19]; none detectable.
  • When to repair (Q5): repair at the first wrong cell beats waiting for three by +8.0 [+7.6, +8.3] at noise 0.125 and +7.3 at 0.15; at noise 0.125 it reaches 229.0 with 35 cell writes per lifetime.
  • Early warning (Q4): damage already present predicts loss within 100 steps (AUC 0.96); classic signals add +0.007; CRR coherence adds −0.0008 [−0.0014, −0.0003]. Frozen prediction missed.

3. Lost structure or never-formed structure: does the tissue remember?

CRR suggested

The "same present, different past" design: compare an eye that was lost with one that never formed, under identical noise. And ask whether stale cell memory blocks repair, as it did in the sorting arrays.

Found

The cells' memory is rebuilt from current input every step, so it cannot go stale. Lost and never-formed eyes converge within a few dozen steps. The paper's injection favours the just-lost eye only at the moment of loss. Ageing needs noise. The paper's targeted repair reproduces.

CRR enabled

The design (matched present, varied history). It made no prediction the ordinary contracting-dynamics model does not make. Grade: QUESTION/DESIGN.

Look closer: numbers and grades of the paper's claims
  • Convergence: lost and never-formed eyes converge in 48/50 lifetimes (median 38.5 steps) at step 300, 28/30 at step 600; neither regrows by itself (0/50, 0/30).
  • Injection: at the moment of loss the just-lost eye regrows more often, 12/50 against 0/50 (p = 0.0005); after any delay of 10 to 200 steps, 0/50 against 0/50.
  • Memory wipe: one step after wiping every cell's memory the difference is 0.003 on average (1% of typical size), 0.037 at most; no cell type changes. Keeping memory during repair equals clearing it (5/5 equivalent).
  • Noise: no decline and no organ lost at noise 0 or 0.025; decline rises with noise (Spearman ρ = 0.93).
  • Paper's repair: targeted repair beats none by +15.3 [13.5, 17.1]: REPRODUCED.

Grades of the paper's claims: ageing "even without noise" FAILS for progressive ageing (holds for one-time settling after development); "memory of lost structures … reactivated" FAILS beyond a few steps; targeted rejuvenation REPRODUCED. Self-sorting arrays and algotypes

Zhang, Goldstein and Levin turned textbook sorting algorithms inside out: each number becomes a "cell" that applies the rule from its own point of view. Arrays mixing cells that run different algorithms ("algotypes") cluster by type for a while. With some cells frozen, the paper reports more "delayed gratification", moves that make things temporarily worse in order to get round a defect. The record now holds two independent audits. One used the authors' own code (P23-ALGOTYPES, 16 October); one reimplemented their rules and then also ran their code (MP-REBUS checks C5–C8, 14 October). Where they overlap, the authors'-code result leads.

Zhang, T., Goldstein, A., & Levin, M. (2025). Classical sorting algorithms as a model of morphogenesis: self-sorting arrays reveal unexpected competencies in a minimal model of basal intelligence. Adaptive Behavior 33:25–54. arXiv:2401.05375. Authors' code: github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research at 1fd2bd59, run unchanged.

Same present, different past: repair after an injury

CRR suggested

M7 says lasting forms are products of their history. So: take two arrays identical in every value and algotype, one sorted by history and one freshly assembled, injure both the same way, and see if they repair the same way.

Found

They do not. No history copy re-sorted; every fresh copy did. The whole effect sits in stored state: selection cells' position pointers, which only advance and overshoot after an injury. Renewing pointers only in the injured window repairs exactly as well as rebuilding.

CRR enabled

The design. An ordinary hidden-state (Markov) model accounts for all of it; its engineering name is cache invalidation. Grade: REDUCES to hidden state; ND-2 unchanged.

Look closer

Authors' classes: history 0/20 for both mixes (36,042 swaps in 30 s, livelock; 15.1, deadlock), fresh 20/20 (80.1 and 72.6 swaps). Mechanism: after the injury, 88 of 89 displaced selection cells still point at their old destination. Source: sweet-gauss:studies/P23-ALGOTYPES-2026-10-16/REPORT.md:238-304. Delayed gratification, corrected

CRR suggested

Measure distance to the goal on a declared ruler, and count steps backwards: surplus = 2 × backward travel. The demand that the ruler be declared is CRR's.

Found

On the true distance to sorted order, no bubble or insertion swap ever goes backwards: 1,613,934 steps, 0 exceptions. "Delayed gratification" appears only on the paper's adjacent-pair measure. The official statistic returns a sum instead of a mean in exactly the frozen-cell runs that stop unsorted. Corrected, the frozen-cell trend disappears, and a strategy-free null matches the cells.

The carrier-relativity distinction (grade DISTINCTION in the Platonic-space note). The trend itself: REDUCES to a measurement artefact; the paper's rising trend FAILS to reproduce.

Algotype clustering

Chimeric clustering is real: every mixed array beats its matched inert-label control, by +0.07 to +0.19 at the peak (Welch t 10.6–27.8). But the peaks are 0.04–0.15 below the paper's, whose values and control match the mean of each run's own maximum rather than the peak of the mean curve. A tempo difference alone, with one identical rule and inert labels, gives the same size of transient clustering (0.58–0.63). And there is no 0.69 "setpoint": final segregation runs from 0.44 to 0.78 with block size and array length. The persistence comes from mixing a stable sort with a non-stable one.

Where CRR led to the standard mathematics

Across this work, CRR's vocabulary kept pointing at tools the relevant field already has. That is a modest result and a real one: the questions were well aimed. Each card names the established result the record says CRR reduces to.

Inversion counting

Surplus on an adjacent-swap sort is twice the moves away from sorted order; on the inversion count, bubble and insertion never move away at all. Knuth, TAOCP vol. 3 §5.1.1.

CRR's view of Platonic space

Here CRR and Levin genuinely differ, and the difference is held openly.

Levin: ingression

In "Ingressing Minds" (Philosophies, 2026) patterns that shape form and function "inhabit a space very different from our physical world"; bodies are "interfaces" for them to "ingress". Levin declines the ontology question and judges the view by its "fecundity". The record finds no equations or operational quantity in it.

CRR: production (M7)

Lasting forms are "productions of realised history: stable relational constraints made by finite events/choices and inherited forward". This is a declared metaphysical position, "not derived by CORE/MATHEMATICS.md".

Not decidable, as things stand. If Levin's space of patterns learns from use, it produces the same sequence of forms as CRR's history, for every experiment anyone could run. The register records this as ND-2. What both views share, and what may be claimed, is history dependence: systems with stable forms and memory of which one they are in.

The fork that would decide it. A later check (C3) separated the views by intervention instead of by statistics. A Platonic library is distinguishable from history-production only if it is shared across physically disconnected instances, or survives physical erasure of an instance's record. An instance-local library that can be reset behaves exactly as a physical memory: it is M7 in other words. Levin's 2026 text leaves exactly those two properties open as questions.

The Fields–Glazebrook–Levin framework is formally closer to CRR than Levin's space is, and in three places more developed: a record-built internal clock, Landauer costs, and an incompleteness theorem for self-models. The sharp disagreement is about the present. Their clock counts writes; CRR's counts distinguishable change (check C1b).
