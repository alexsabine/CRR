CRR, Levin and the Origin of Forms

Michael Levin's work treats the growth of a body as problem-solving: groups of cells finding their way to a target shape, and remembering it. A flatworm briefly treated with a drug can regenerate with two heads, and keep that two-headed plan through later cuts. Levin has also proposed that some forms are not made from scratch but "ingress" from a structured space of patterns.

Those are questions about memory, history and where forms come from, which are CRR's questions too. This page is an honest record of where a CRR reading of Levin's work led. In short: each time, CRR's vocabulary pointed at a piece of established mathematics that already answers the question. CRR gave those results names; it did not add new numbers. One real disagreement remains, about where forms come from. It is stated here as a disagreement, held openly, with the one test that could bear on part of it.

Where CRR led us to the standard mathematics

A CRR reading asks the same few questions of any system: how far did it travel, how far did it really get, what did turning back cost, and what does it rebuild from. Asked of Levin's examples, those questions led each time to a tool that the relevant field already has. That is a modest result and a real one: the vocabulary pointed in the right direction. It did not go beyond what the field already knew.

Sorting, and the cost of a step back

In a 40-cell sorting array, CRR's "surplus" (swaps taken minus the fewest possible) came out exactly twice the number of moves away from sorted order: 0, 2 and 10 for 0, 1 and 5 such moves. This is the inversion-count identity: every adjacent swap changes the number of out-of-order pairs by exactly one.

Grade: REDUNDANT-DOMAIN. "Knuth has it. CRR adds a name."

Look closer

Numbers (Ashes ontology/checks/turing_safety_ingression.txt:57): p = 0: swaps C = 427, inversions C* = 427, S = 0, away-moves 0. p = 0.1: C = 429, S = 2, away-moves 1. p = 0.3: C = 437, S = 10, away-moves 5. A rival "chord" (straight-line displacement of the cells, 109.8271) gives surplus 317.1729 to 327.1729, so the array's own unit does the work. Identity holds at every p: True.

Standard result: swaps − inversions = 2 × (inversion-increasing swaps); Knuth, The Art of Computer Programming vol. 3, §5.1.1. Reading: "every move away from the goal costs two swaps; the domain has it as an exact identity and CRR adds the name" (txt:63). Note: Ashes ontology/07_turing_safety_ingression.md:92-99.

Script: turing_safety_ingression.py, row 4. More in the next section.

A form that was found, not stored

A Hopfield network stores three patterns, yet sometimes settles into a fourth that was never stored: their majority blend. The battery read it as an "ingressed" form, in Levin's sense. CRR's rule for rebuilding from the past (an average of what has settled) returns exactly that blend. Amit, Gutfreund and Sompolinsky wrote it down in 1985.

Grade: REDUNDANT-DOMAIN. "The two metaphysics share every number."

Look closer

Numbers (Ashes turing_safety_ingression.txt:122): N = 100 units, P = 3 stored patterns, 400 random starts. Settled in a stored pattern or its negative: 327; in the three-pattern mixture or its negative: 20; elsewhere: 53. Overlap of CRR's equal-weight average with the 1985 mixture state sign(ξ₁ + ξ₂ + ξ₃): 1.0000. With age weights favouring the newest pattern: 0.3600. The mixture is a fixed point: True.

The note adds a caution: "which form CRR says is regenerated depends on which weights it gives the past, and it has not said" (ontology/07_turing_safety_ingression.md:101-120).

Script: turing_safety_ingression.py, row 8.

Which form a symmetric system settles into

When a law allows two equal forms, the law cannot say which one appears. In 2000 simulated histories, the early path named the final form 98.1% of the time; the law alone does no better than chance. This is symmetry breaking under Curie's principle: the answer to "why this form?" lives in the history.

Grade: formal check, no rung. Named by the record as standard.

Look closer

Numbers (Phoenix studies/FORMS-AND-EASEMENT-2026-10-06/forms_output.json): F1a, the law gives P(form +) = 0.5145 over 2000 histories; F1b, the sign of the path at a tenth of the run predicts the realised form with accuracy 0.981; F1c, must-fail arm, with a tilted law the form is predictable from the law alone (1.0). F2, hysteresis: a settled form absorbs kicks up to 1.0 (the distance to the separatrix) and is replaced beyond it.

The record: "in every case that content is a known piece of mathematics" (FORMS_AND_EASEMENT_PART1-2.md:25,33). This result is about form selection. It is not about ageing.

Precedence: forms that get easier to repeat

If forms are made by history, a form that has happened before should become more likely to happen again, so the next form gets more predictable as precedents pile up. In a toy model the uncertainty fell from 3.00 to 2.54 bits over 60 occasions; with a fixed library of forms it stayed at 3.00. This is Lee Smolin's principle of precedence.

Grade: formal check, no rung. CRR "shares a prediction here rather than owning one".

Look closer

Numbers (forms_output.json, F4): eight candidate forms, a Pólya urn for production (each occurrence makes the form more likely), 400 trials; entropy 3.0 → 2.53932209050007 bits after 59 precedents; fixed library 3.0 → 3.0. Source for the principle: Smolin, arXiv:1205.3707 (FORMS_AND_EASEMENT_PART3-4.md:14).

The catch: "Let the library's weights update with use and the two are equivalent" (FORMS_AND_EASEMENT_PART1-2.md:30). See CRR's view of Platonic space.

Script: forms_checks.py, check F4.

Ageing damage: no contact

A 2026 model of ageing treats death as damage first crossing a threshold. CRR asked whether the total distance the damage had wandered predicts death beyond the current damage level. It does not: within a narrow band of damage, the wandering scored 0.42 (0.5 is chance), while the damage level itself scored 0.90. There is no ageing-reversal or recovery result anywhere in the record.

Grade: NO CONTACT. The model is Markov; its current state already holds what matters.

Convergence: others reached the same objects

Two 2026 papers, independently of CRR, built the same kind of object. Taye's ageing model measures a body's own "biological proper time" by integrating a physiological rate over time, which is CRR's clock of becoming. A planarian model by Blattner uses CRR's surplus-and-chord pair as hidden regenerative memory. This suggests the ideas are natural; it is not evidence that CRR is right or new.

Grades: Taye, REPHRASE ("same construction"); Blattner, REPHRASE with one transferable constraint ("same equation").

Delayed gratification and algotypes

Zhang, Goldstein and Levin turned a textbook sorting algorithm inside out: instead of one program moving numbers around, each element acts like a cell and applies the sorting rule from its own point of view. When some cells are frozen and cannot move, the array still sorts, sometimes by moving temporarily away from sorted order to get round the defect. They call such moves "delayed gratification".

Zhang, T., Goldstein, A., & Levin, M. (2025). Classical sorting algorithms as a model of morphogenesis: self-sorting arrays reveal unexpected competencies in a minimal model of basal intelligence. Adaptive Behavior. doi:10.1177/10597123241269740; preprint arXiv:2401.05375. The record holds this paper only as a search snippet (its use there: "cell-view sorting, frozen cells, 'delayed gratification' moves"); the full text was not read, and the summary above goes no further than that. The term "algotype" appears nowhere in Phoenix or Ashes, and nothing on this page tests the paper's algotype results.

What was actually run

A 40-cell array in a random order. In each pass every cell compares itself with its right-hand neighbour and swaps if the pair is out of order. One cell, in the middle, sometimes swaps the wrong way, with probability p = 0, 0.1 or 0.3. CRR counts every swap taken (C) and the fewest swaps that could have done the job, which is the number of out-of-order pairs at the start (C*). The difference is the surplus S.

The result: S = 2 × (moves away), exactly, at every p. A step away from the goal costs two swaps: the step itself and the step that undoes it. That is a known identity about inversions in a list, so the record grades it REDUNDANT-DOMAIN and says plainly: "Knuth has it. CRR adds a name, and the name is a good one for teaching: every step away costs two."

Why this is not yet a reproduction

  • The paper was not read in full. Only a search snippet and the citation were available.
  • The defect is different. Here a cell swaps the wrong way at random; in the paper, cells are frozen. The identity does not depend on that choice, but the experiment is not theirs.
  • None of the paper's own measures was computed. No frozen-cell runs, no algotype or mixed-algorithm arrays, no clustering, no delayed-gratification statistic in their sense.

A faithful reproduction, with frozen cells, algotypes and the paper's own measure of delayed gratification, has never been attempted and is listed as pending.

CRR's view of Platonic space

Here CRR and Levin genuinely differ, and the difference is worth stating carefully.

Ingression

Levin entertains a structured, non-physical space of patterns that can enter ("ingress" into) physical bodies; some patterns may have agency. On this view a form exists before the event and the event points to it.

Production

CRR's commitment M7 says the reverse: lasting forms are made by what has actually happened, as constraints laid down by finite events and carried forward. The event makes the form.

"A CRR-friendly alternative to separate Platonic space is that persistent forms are productions of realised history: stable relational constraints made by finite events/choices and inherited forward. This is the user's metaphysical proposal. It is not derived by CORE/MATHEMATICS.md, and it must not be presented as if it were."M7, CONSTITUTION/METAPHYSICAL_COMMITMENTS.md:23-26

The record calls this "a real metaphysical disagreement, not a terminological difference" (FOUNDATIONS/LEVIN_WATSON.md:62). M7 is a declared position, not a result. The author's own reading is Winnicott's paradox: forms are both created and discovered.

Can evidence decide it? Not as ontology

If Levin's space of patterns can itself learn, so that forms which have appeared become more likely to appear again, then it produces exactly the same sequence of forms as CRR's history does, for every experiment anyone could run. The register of undecidable questions records this as ND-2: not decidable. What both views share, and what may be claimed, is history dependence: systems with two or more stable forms, and memory of which one they are in.

The one place they differ observably

Against a fixed library of forms, production does make a different prediction: precedence. The uncertainty about the next form should fall as forms repeat. That prediction is Lee Smolin's principle of precedence (2012), which CRR shares rather than owns, and a library that learns from use makes it too.

What the planarian record already says

There is no public per-animal dataset from the Levin lab, so only published summary numbers could be read. Two-headed worms stay two-headed through recuts (100% over three generations), and the "cryptic" worms keep a steady 23% to 27% two-headed outcome rather than drifting. Both of the memory rules CRR's commitments had been read as (a fading memory, or a precedence that keeps accumulating) predict otherwise, and both are ruled out. What fits is the Levin lab's own model of a switch with two stable states, which is "dynamical-systems theory, not CRR's".

A test that could decide part of it

Phoenix has declared one discriminating experiment, P23-LEVIN1. Does a settled two-headed history in a flatworm change the outcome of a different switch, the head shape induced by octanol? The rival that can win is the lab's modular model, in which the two switches are separate circuits. Even a positive result would show history dependence for this pair of switches, at modest specificity; it would not settle the ontology.

It is a declaration only: not frozen, not run. It needs a laboratory partner. Later review also found design flaws, listed below, and the test stays at its current stage until they are fixed.

Fading memory predicts decay toward single-headed: wrong. Accumulating precedence predicts a rising fraction: wrong (stationary). Bistable switch: compatible. Retrodictive, on seen data (rung R5 at most).

P23-LEVIN1 design (DECLARATION.md):

  • Claim: a stable two-headed history in Dugesia japonica changes the distribution of octanol-induced head shapes.
  • Rival: the modular bioelectric-circuit reading (Durant 2017, Fig. S6).
  • Prediction: P(shape | two-headed history) ≠ P(shape | no history), exact test of homogeneity, α = 0.05, direction free; at least 60 naive animals per group; shape scored blind to history.
  • Controls: two no-history cohorts must not differ; head-shape induction must reproduce Emmons-Bell 2015 Fig. 3B within its 95% intervals; 100% two-headed on a confirmatory recut.
  • Ceiling: specificity S2 at most; "never a claim about forms as ontology".

Status. Declaration, not frozen, not run; the enquiry registry still reads "NONE — proposal only". Review verdict STAYS_AT_CURRENT_STAGE (ENQUIRIES/FLAME_CHECK_2026-10-08_assessments.json, P23).

Blockers named in the declaration (DECLARATION.md:19): Levin 2026 read in full; two open decisions on M7 by the author; a laboratory partner with a data-sharing agreement; a fit of the memory rule to the published series; a frozen scorer and surrogate battery; a power calculation.

Design flaws found at review (FLAME check, P23 blockers): the history is not assigned (two-headedness arises by chance after the drug, 25% two-headed against 72% cryptic, so responders select themselves); octanol drives both switches; there is no exposure-matched cryptic arm; the reference frequencies exist "only in a graph and from a different species"; there is no minimum detectable effect or power; the collaboration licence is unsigned; Watson's natural induction is not yet formalised in its own terms.
