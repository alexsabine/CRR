Coherence–Rupture–Regeneration

Mathematical Foundations for a Temporal Process Theory

A. Sabine · Version 6.0 · March 2026

What is CRR? A Plain-Language Introduction

The Core Idea

You don't experience time as a smooth flow—you experience distinct "nows," each one feeling complete before giving way to the next. William James called these "perchings" and "flights" of consciousness.

CRR proposes that this pattern—build up, break, rebuild—may be a useful way to describe how bounded systems navigate time. It draws on process philosophy (Whitehead, 1929): reality is not made of things that change, but of changes that occasionally cohere into things.

The Three Phases

Coherence: The Accumulated Past

Think of coherence as "distance travelled." As you read this sentence, your beliefs shift—words build on words, meaning moves. Mathematically, coherence is the length of the path your beliefs have walked since the last transition (Fisher–Rao arc length, in units of a resolution $\sigma$), counting every step, including steps back.

Rupture: The Decisive Present

When the system first arrives at the point opposite where it began—the half-turn of its state space—transformation occurs. This is rupture: the discrete, instantaneous moment when the system reorganises. The key idea: rupture happens when the product of the distance from where it began ($C^*$) and $\Omega$ reaches 1.

Regeneration: The Reconstructed Future

After rupture, the system rebuilds using its history—but not all history equally. Recent occasions count more, and the proposal (still untested) is that occasions which took more turning back to complete count more too. Memory is weighted by recency and, perhaps, by significance.

The Key Numbers: $\Omega$ and $q$

$\Omega$ is not a dial. It is fixed by the geometry: $\Omega = 1/a$, where $a$ is the half-turn, the distance from a starting point to the point opposite it. For a two-state (Bernoulli) system, and for a circle of circumference $2\pi$, $\Omega = 1/\pi$.

The dial is the recency $q$, between 0 and 1:

  • $q$ near 0 (habit): the system rebuilds almost entirely from its most recent occasion. Think: crystallised skill, routines, loops.
  • $q$ near 1 (long memory): the system draws on the long past. Think: reconsidering, healing, a change of mind that remembers where it came from.

CRR is not parameter-free. The free choices are the resolution $\sigma$, the overall scale of the Fisher metric, and the recency $q$.

First Principles

CRR rests on a small set of axioms drawn from information geometry, distribution theory and process philosophy. Each borrows from established mathematics; the framework built on them is a proposal, not an established result.

Axiom I: Coherence

All systems that persist accumulate evidence through time
Coherence since the last cut
$$C(t) = \frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{t_n}^{t} L(\tau)\, d\tau$$
The Fisher–Rao speed
$$L(\tau) = \sqrt{g(\theta_\tau)}\;\lvert\dot\theta_\tau\rvert$$

$\theta$ is the system's state on a circle of probability distributions, and $g$ is the Fisher information: how distinguishable neighbouring states are. $\sigma$ is a resolution, calibrated separately: the smallest change the system can tell apart. $t_n$ is the last rupture. $C$ counts every step taken, including steps back, in units of $\sigma$.

Any bounded system that maintains itself against dissipation keeps updating its beliefs. $C$ measures how far those beliefs have travelled, not how good they are: it grows whenever beliefs move, whether or not the move improves the fit. Read in Free Energy Principle terms, it is the path length of belief updating since the last cut, not the amount of free energy reduced.

Fisher Information and the Cramér-Rao Bound

Fisher information measures the curvature of the log-likelihood: how sharply the data distinguish between nearby hypotheses.

The Cramér-Rao inequality then states a fundamental limit:

$$\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq \frac{1}{I(\theta)} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \sigma^2 \cdot I(\theta) \geq 1$$

No unbiased estimator can have variance smaller than the inverse of the accumulated Fisher information. This is not a modelling assumption—it is a theorem of mathematical statistics, proven independently by Cramér (1946) and Rao (1945). Ito & Dechant (2020) extended this to stochastic thermodynamics, showing that the Cramér-Rao bound governs the trade-off between current fluctuations and entropy production in irreversible processes far from equilibrium.

Axiom II: Rupture

Coherence cannot accumulate indefinitely: a temporal boundary is required
The cut
$$\delta(t-t_{n+1})$$
When it happens: the first arrival at $P_1$
$$t_{n+1} = \inf\{t > t_n : C^*\Omega = 1\}$$

$C^*$ is the geodesic distance from where the occasion began, and $\Omega$ = 1/(the manifold's diameter), both in units of $\sigma$. The surplus $S = C - C^* \geq 0$ is zero only when the path never turns back.

No system can build coherence without limit. CRR models this boundary with the Dirac delta—an instantaneous moment of transformation.

The Temporal Markov Blanket

In the FEP, a Markov blanket is a spatial boundary that renders internal states conditionally independent of external states. CRR proposes that the Dirac delta $\delta(\text{now})$ serves as the temporal analogue: the boundary between past and future, between coherence and regeneration.

The delta has three properties:

  • Unit mass: $\int\delta(t)\,dt = 1$. This is not adjustable; it is a definitional property of the distribution.
  • Homogeneity: $\delta(at) = \delta(t)/|a|$. The delta has no width of its own, so it picks out no timescale.
  • Conditional independence (proposed): CRR proposes that future states (regeneration) are conditionally independent of past states (coherence) given what is carried across the cut. This would be a Markov property in time rather than space; it is a modelling choice, not a property of the delta itself.

The Dirac delta distributes its unit mass across the boundary between inside (all past states—coherence accumulated within the blanket) and outside (all future states—regeneration beyond the blanket). The present moment is where inside becomes outside; where evidence becomes action; where the accumulated past becomes the reconstructed future.

Axiom III: Regeneration

Systems persist through transformation, not despite it
The new state: a weighted (Fréchet) mean of what each occasion settled
$$m_{n+1} = \arg\min_{y} \sum_{j=-1}^{n} w_j \, d(y, \Phi_j)^2$$
The weight of each occasion: recency × salience
$$w_j = q^{\,n-j} \cdot e^{S_j\,\Omega}$$

$\Phi_j$ is what occasion $j$ settled, $\Phi_{-1} = m_0$ is the inherited past, from before the system began, and $d$ is distance on the content space. For scalar content this is just the weighted average $\sum_j w_j \Phi_j / \sum_j w_j$; on a circle the mean respects wraparound and can be non-unique. Recency $q^{n-j}$ (with $0 \le q \lt 1$, no universal value): $q$ near 0 rebuilds from the latest occasion (habit), $q$ near 1 draws on the long past. Salience $e^{S_j\Omega}$ is the surplus $S_j = C_j - C_j^*$ measured in half-turns ($\sigma$ cancels): occasions that turned back more before reaching the cut weigh more. Salience is a proposal still to be tested.

After rupture, the system reconstructs from memory. $\Omega$ is fixed by the geometry and converts surplus into half-turns; how far back memory reaches is set by the recency $q$.

Axiom IV: Unity

At the moment of transformation: $C^* \cdot \Omega = 1$

The cut uses the chord $C^*$, not the arc length $C$: the occasion ends on first arrival at the point opposite where it began.

1. Standing Assumptions

The following technical assumptions ensure mathematical well-posedness.

Assumption (A1) Regularity

For each $x$ and $t$, the function $\tau \mapsto \mathcal{L}(x,\tau)$ is locally integrable on $[0,t]$.

Assumption (A2) Non-negativity

$\mathcal{L}(x,\tau) \geq 0$ for all $x, \tau$. Arc length only accumulates; steps back add to it rather than subtracting.

Assumption (A3) Bounded Rate (No-Zeno Condition)

There exists $M > 0$ such that $\mathcal{L}(x,t) \leq M$ for all $x, t$. This ensures each coherence cycle has duration at least $\sigma/(M\Omega)$, preventing infinitely many ruptures in finite time.

2. Core Definitions

Definition 1 (Coherence Accumulation Rate)

The coherence accumulation rate is a function $\mathcal{L} : \mathcal{X} \times [0,T] \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ that assigns to each state-time pair $(x,\tau)$ the speed at which the system's beliefs are moving.

Dimensions: $[\mathcal{L}] = [T^{-1}]$ (a rate, so that $\frac{1}{\sigma}\int \mathcal{L}\, d\tau$ is dimensionless).

Identification: $\mathcal{L}$ is the Fisher–Rao speed, $L(\tau) = \sqrt{g(\theta_\tau)}\;\lvert\dot\theta_\tau\rvert$.

Definition 2 (Coherence)

Let $t_n$ denote the most recent rupture time before $t$ (with $t_0 = 0$). The coherence at time $t$ is the Fisher–Rao arc length travelled since the last rupture:

Coherence since the last cut
$$C(t) = \frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{t_n}^{t} L(\tau)\, d\tau$$
The Fisher–Rao speed
$$L(\tau) = \sqrt{g(\theta_\tau)}\;\lvert\dot\theta_\tau\rvert$$

$\theta$ is the system's state on a circle of probability distributions, and $g$ is the Fisher information: how distinguishable neighbouring states are. $\sigma$ is a resolution, calibrated separately: the smallest change the system can tell apart. $t_n$ is the last rupture. $C$ counts every step taken, including steps back, in units of $\sigma$.

Properties: Dimensionless, monotone non-decreasing within each cycle, resets to 0 at each rupture. The chord $C^*$ (geodesic distance from where the occasion began, also in units of $\sigma$) satisfies $C^* \leq C$; the surplus $S = C - C^* \geq 0$ is the cost of turning back.

Definition 3 ($\Omega$)

$\Omega = 1/a$, where $a$ is the diameter of the state manifold (the half-turn: the distance to the point opposite the start), in units of $\sigma$. $\Omega$ is fixed by the geometry of the carrier; it is not a tunable parameter.

The cut condition $C^* \cdot \Omega = 1$ (Axiom IV) says that the chord has reached the diameter: $C^* = a = 1/\Omega$.

Definition 4 (Rupture)

A rupture occurs at time $t_{n+1}$ when the chord $C^*$ first reaches the diameter $1/\Omega$:

The cut
$$\delta(t-t_{n+1})$$
When it happens: the first arrival at $P_1$
$$t_{n+1} = \inf\{t > t_n : C^*\Omega = 1\}$$

$C^*$ is the geodesic distance from where the occasion began, and $\Omega$ = 1/(the manifold's diameter), both in units of $\sigma$. The surplus $S = C - C^* \geq 0$ is zero only when the path never turns back.

Following rupture: $C(t_{n+1}^+) = 0$.

Definition 5 (Regeneration)

The regeneration operator reconstructs the system state:

The new state: a weighted (Fréchet) mean of what each occasion settled
$$m_{n+1} = \arg\min_{y} \sum_{j=-1}^{n} w_j \, d(y, \Phi_j)^2$$
The weight of each occasion: recency × salience
$$w_j = q^{\,n-j} \cdot e^{S_j\,\Omega}$$

$\Phi_j$ is what occasion $j$ settled, $\Phi_{-1} = m_0$ is the inherited past, from before the system began, and $d$ is distance on the content space. For scalar content this is just the weighted average $\sum_j w_j \Phi_j / \sum_j w_j$; on a circle the mean respects wraparound and can be non-unique. Recency $q^{n-j}$ (with $0 \le q \lt 1$, no universal value): $q$ near 0 rebuilds from the latest occasion (habit), $q$ near 1 draws on the long past. Salience $e^{S_j\Omega}$ is the surplus $S_j = C_j - C_j^*$ measured in half-turns ($\sigma$ cancels): occasions that turned back more before reaching the cut weigh more. Salience is a proposal still to be tested.

Key Insight: Weighted Memory

History is weighted by recency and, on the untested salience proposal, by how much each occasion turned back. This is consonant with Bergson's insight that memory is "the continuous presence of history," not retrieval from storage.

3. Symmetry Classification

The simplest carriers are one-dimensional: states on a circle $S^1$ of probability distributions, or on a segment such as the Bernoulli family.

Symmetry Classes

For a manifold of diameter $a$ (in units of $\sigma$), the cut is the first arrival at the point opposite the start:

$$\Omega = \frac{1}{a}$$
  • $\mathbb{Z}_2$ / Bernoulli (bistable): with the standard Fisher normalisation the diameter is $\pi$, so $\Omega = 1/\pi \approx 0.318$ and $C^* = \pi$ at the cut.
  • Circle / $SO(2)$ (rotational): the cut is the half-turn, so the diameter is half the circumference, $a = \ell/2$. For a circle of circumference $2\pi$ this again gives $\Omega = 1/\pi$. There is no factor of 2 between the classes; the circumference itself depends on the statistical carrier.

On a circle, the surplus is twice the distance backtracked; uneven speed adds nothing.

4. Key Properties

Lemma (No Zeno Pathology)

Under (A3), $C^* \leq C \leq M(t-t_n)/\sigma$, so each coherence cycle has duration at least $\sigma/(M\Omega)$. Hence the number of ruptures in any finite interval $[0,T]$ is at most $\lfloor TM\Omega/\sigma \rfloor + 1 \lt \infty$.

1. The Regeneration Mechanism

In Plain Language

When a system regenerates after rupture, it doesn't treat all of history equally. Recent occasions count more; and, on a proposal still to be tested, occasions that took more turning back to complete count more too.

Salience is measured within each occasion, in its own terms: how much extra path it walked beyond the straight chord to the cut.

1.1 Weight Function

Definition (Occasion Weight)

The unnormalised weight:

The weight of each occasion: recency × salience
$$w_j = q^{\,n-j} \cdot e^{S_j\,\Omega}$$

1.2 The Recency Dial $q$

Remark

$\Omega$ is fixed by the geometry, so it is not the dial. The dial is the recency $q$ ($0 \le q \lt 1$, no universal value):

  • $q$ near 0 (habit): the system rebuilds almost entirely from its latest occasion.
  • $q$ near 1 (long memory): the system draws on the long past, including the inherited $m_0$.

The salience factor $e^{S_j\Omega}$ only reads surplus in half-turns; it does not set how far back memory reaches.

2. Non-Markovian Signature

Remark (Path Dependence)

Two systems with identical current coherence but different histories will in general have different regenerations. This follows directly from $m_{n+1}$ depending on the full history $\{\Phi_j, S_j\}_{j=-1}^{n}$, not just the current value $C(t)$.

What This Means

History matters, not just its summary. Two systems with the same current state but different histories can respond differently to the same challenge. In the current state alone, this looks non-Markovian: the regenerated state depends on the weighted past, not merely the present.

3. $\Omega$ in Both Rupture and Regeneration

$\Omega$ appears in the rupture condition ($C^* \cdot \Omega = 1$) and in the salience factor ($e^{S_j\Omega}$, surplus counted in half-turns). It answers two questions in the same units:

  • "When does the system transform?" → When $C^* \cdot \Omega = 1$, i.e. when the chord $C^*$ first reaches the diameter $1/\Omega$
  • "How does it remember?" → Through $q^{\,n-j} \cdot e^{S_j\Omega}$ weighting of the full history

CRR and the Free Energy Principle

What is the Free Energy Principle?

The FEP (Friston, 2010; 2019) proposes that living systems survive by minimising "free energy"—the mismatch between what they expect and experience. CRR offers a vocabulary for temporal structure: when might beliefs update? How might accumulated history shape reconstitution?

The Temporal Gap in the FEP

The FEP's primary temporal apparatus is generalised coordinates of motion (Friston, 2008)—a vector of higher-order time derivatives that encodes local trajectory information. This is elegant for continuous dynamics within a regime, but it remains fundamentally local in time: each state depends only on its current generalised coordinates, preserving the Markov property. Biehl, Pollock & Kanai (2021) identified technical difficulties with this formulation.

The FEP's path integral formulation (Friston, 2019) scores the plausibility of entire trajectories, but still does not specify when a system must abandon one regime for another, nor how the transition draws on accumulated history. The FEP tells you that a system at nonequilibrium steady state will look as if it is performing inference. It does not tell you the timing of the inference, or the moment at which the current model is exhausted.

Both frameworks use information geometry, but CRR's $\Omega$ is not the FEP's precision: $\Omega$ is fixed by the diameter of the state space, whereas precision is a quantity inferred or learned within a generative model.

Rupture and Bayesian Model Reduction

The "Aha Moment" Correspondence

In Friston et al. (2025) "Active Inference and Artificial Reasoning," an "aha moment" occurs when evidence accumulates until confidence exceeds a threshold, triggering Bayesian Model Reduction (BMR). CRR suggests a candidate geometric criterion for when such a transition happens: $C^* \cdot \Omega = 1$. Whether this matches the timing of BMR in practice is untested.

A Suggested Correspondence

Rupture may be read as the "aha moment." Both describe a discrete transition from uncertainty to commitment. CRR suggests a geometric criterion for when it happens, $C^* \cdot \Omega = 1$. It makes no claim about how variable the timing is: that depends on the dynamics.

What CRR Does Not Replace

CRR does not compete with the FEP's account of what beliefs update to (free energy minimisation), nor with the detailed neural process theories (predictive coding, active inference) that implement it. CRR tries to describe the temporal structure of these processes: when transitions occur, and how history shapes reconstitution. It is offered as a vocabulary for when transitions happen; the dynamics themselves come from the models.

The Record So Far

An honest tally, as of this version. Of 197 readings of known results, 0 were sharp, 41 consistent, 86 descriptive, 20 failed, 9 were in tension and 41 remain open. Most were made with the answer already known, so they illustrate the vocabulary rather than test it.

On pre-registered tests against unseen data: 16 passes (all in machine learning, each matching established methods, never beating them), 37 failures (plus 8 more for a proposed universal regeneration law, which is withdrawn), and 5 void. There has been no independent replication. Further pre-registered checks run in October 2026 (bacterial cell division, memoryless timing, the boundary as a rate, and the Kan-extension reading) are awaiting review before they are added to this tally; the bacterial test reduced to standard size control and measurement noise.

What Was Withdrawn

Earlier versions of this page carried a prediction for the variability of the intervals between ruptures, with a fixed ratio between symmetry classes. It was not derived and has been withdrawn: how variable the intervals are is a first-passage question that depends on each system's dynamics, not on $\Omega$ alone. Some later tests were registered before the data were examined; many earlier readings were not. Failures are reported as failures.
