What you are seeing

A complete graph Kn, where every node connects to every other. Each node is an autonomous agent running an SO(2) cycle§CRR: coherence accumulates along a closed internal loop until C = 2π, then ruptures. In active inference terms, this is an agent with its own generative model, accumulating evidence until a precision-weighted threshold triggers a belief update (FEP). Each edge is a Markov blanket (FEP) running a Z₂ switch§CRR: coherence accumulates across a bistable boundary until C = π, then flips. In both cases, C · Ω = 1 at rupture — the Cramér-Rao bound.

The gold glow is the beauty function§CRR B(C,Ω) = exp(C/Ω) · (1 − C·Ω), peaking at C* − Ω: one fluctuation-width before rupture. In active inference, this corresponds to epistemic value (FEP): the moment of maximum information about the impending transition. For Z₂ blankets this is ~90% through the cycle; for SO(2) agents, ~97.5%.

Agents and blankets

In the free energy principle, a Markov blanket is the set of states separating an agent's internal states from external states. In CRR, each blanket is a Z₂ process: a bistable boundary that accumulates coherence and ruptures at half the threshold of the agent it separates. The blanket's variance ΩZ₂ = 1/π is exactly twice the agent's ΩSO₂ = 1/2π§CRR. This is the cost of autonomy: agents maintain tighter precision at the expense of longer coherence accumulation before rupture.

The quadratic explosion: each new agent must form a blanket with every existing agent. Person 4 doesn't add one blanket; they add three. Person 10 adds nine. Total blankets = n(n−1)/2. At K₃ blankets equal agents (3 = 3); after that, blankets dominate forever.

Individual and group autonomy

Individual autonomy Aind = 1/n§SOLVED: each agent's share of total system variance. Group autonomy Agrp = (n−1)/n: the collective's share, carried by the Markov blankets. These sum to 1.

At K₂: 50/50. At K₁₀: 90% group. At K₂₀: 95% group. Group autonomy measures the degree to which the collective has become autonomous from its own agents. Yet individual autonomy never reaches zero; every agent retains an irreducible internal cycle.

Integration

The Kuramoto order parameter Φ = |1/n · Σ exp(iθⱼ)|, where θⱼ = 2π · C/C* is each agent's coherence phase§CRR. Φ = 1 when all agents are synchronised. Φ ≈ 1/√n when independent. Watch how Φ fluctuates at K₂ (phases drifting in and out), then settles near 1/√n at larger n.

Entrainment

With ε = 0, agents accumulate coherence independently. Raise the slider and each rupture — whether agent or blanket — delivers a kick of ε · Ω to connected agents§CRR. In FEP terms, this is a prediction error propagated through the Markov blanket (FEP).

Crucially, agents near their rupture threshold are more susceptible: the kick is amplified by (1 + 3p²), where p is how far through the coherence cycle the receiving agent is. An agent at 90% receives nearly 3.5× the kick of one at the start. This is critical slowing down: the closer a system is to transition, the more sensitive it becomes to perturbation.

The result is cascades. One agent ruptures, kicking its neighbours through their shared blankets. Those nearest their own thresholds get pushed over, triggering their ruptures, which kick their neighbours. At high ε, watch for waves of red sweeping through the network.

TEMPORAL GRAMMAR · CRR NETWORK · 2025

What changes at scale

Beyond ~20 agents, the network crosses a threshold. You cannot see individual blankets any more; the canvas becomes a mesh. This is not a rendering limitation. At K₅₀ there are 1,225 Markov blankets and no agent can attend to each one. The system's dynamics — its free energy landscape (FEP) — are dominated by the collective, not by any particular boundary.

The quadratic wall

Blankets scale as n(n−1)/2. At K₂₀ there are 190. At K₅₀ there are 1,225. At K₁₅₀ there are 11,175. The ratio of blankets to agents is (n−1)/2, which means K₁₅₀ has 74.5 blankets per agent. Each agent is embedded in 149 simultaneous statistical boundaries. No generative model can track this. The group manages itself.

This is why organisations develop cultures, norms, and institutions: they are compression mechanisms for a blanket space too large for any agent to model. A shared language is blanket coherence made portable. A norm is a Z₂ pattern that ruptures and regenerates faster than any agent can register. An institution is a hierarchical decomposition of the blanket space into tractable sub-networks.

Dunbar's number

Robin Dunbar proposed ~150 as the cognitive limit for stable social relationships. CRR gives this a precise interpretation: at K₁₅₀, individual autonomy is 1/150 ≈ 0.7%§SOLVED. The group carries 99.3% of the system's variance. Beyond this, the blankets are so dominant that adding more agents barely changes the group's dynamics; you need hierarchical structure to maintain any meaningful individual contribution.

In FEP terms: Dunbar's limit marks the point where the free energy of the blanket ensemble so dominates the agents' internal free energy that further scaling requires structural decomposition — nested Markov blankets, departments, clans — to preserve any agent-level inference§PARTIAL.

Entrainment cascades at scale

With ε > 0 at large n, each rupture delivers ε · Ω to connected agents, amplified by their susceptibility: agents near threshold receive up to 3.5× the base kick. At K₁₅₀, each agent has 149 blankets. When several blankets rupture in succession, the cumulative kick — amplified by susceptibility — pushes the agent past its own threshold. Its rupture propagates outward through all 149 of its blankets, perturbing other agents, and so on. The cascade appears as a wave of red.

This is a phase transition: below a critical ε, agents drift independently. Above it, the network locks into collective oscillation. The critical ε decreases with n; larger networks synchronise more easily. In FEP terms, the ensemble has found a shared generative model, minimising collective free energy through blanket-mediated prediction error (FEP).
