Understanding CRR

A proposal: finite systems that persist may accumulate a history, reach their limits, and rebuild from what they have been

These videos were recorded during the development of CRR. The core intuitions hold, but two details have since been refined: the rupture condition is C*·Ω = 1 (not C = Ω), and the Z₂/SO(2) symmetry class structure had not yet emerged. The page below reflects the current framework. The author's understanding has, in other words, undergone its own rupture.

Mathematics of Becoming

Most mathematical models of change assume a system's future depends only on its present state. This is the Markov assumption, and it is extraordinarily useful. But it misses something that living systems know: the present carries the weight of the past. A person living with trauma cannot simply decide to be different. An ecosystem recovering from fire does not snap back. A child's early attention shapes what they can later perceive. The history is not background; it is load-bearing structure.

CRR is a temporal grammar that takes this seriously. The coherence integral makes the system's state explicitly dependent on its regime's entire history: non-Markovian by design. The rupture condition says that this accumulation cannot continue without limit, because the manifold is finite and has a measurable geodesic extent. And regeneration says that after rupture, the system rebuilds from its own history.

Five Commitments

Commitment 1

Persistence implies accumulation

A system that persists must accumulate patterns that work. This is formalised as a coherence integral: the accumulated arc length along the system's information-geometric trajectory.

Coherence since the last cutC(t) = (1/σ) ∫tnt L(τ) dτσ is a resolution, calibrated separately: the smallest change the system can tell apart. tn is the last rupture. C counts every step taken, including steps back, in units of σ. The Fisher–Rao speedL(τ) = √g(θτ) · |θ̇τ|θ is the system's state on a circle of probability distributions, and g is the Fisher information: how distinguishable neighbouring states are. The integral over the system's past makes CRR non-Markovian by design. The present state depends on the accumulated history of the current regime. This is the interior of the temporal Markov blanket.
Commitment 2

Finite capacity implies rupture

No finite system can accumulate without bound. The manifold has a measurable geodesic extent, and a system traversing it must eventually span it. In this model, reorganisation follows from the geometry.

The cutδ(t − tn+1)The Dirac delta has zero temporal extension and carries one unit of mass on the boundary between past and future. When it happens: the first arrival at P1tn+1 = inf{ t > tn : C*Ω = 1 }C* is the geodesic distance from where the occasion began, and Ω = 1/(the manifold's diameter), both in units of σ. The surplus S = C − C* ≥ 0 is zero only when the path never turns back.
Commitment 3

Reconstruction is weighted by history: recency and salience

After rupture, the system rebuilds from its own history. History is never lost, only selectively weighted. The past is never repeated identically; it is transformed.

The new state: a weighted memory of what each occasion settledmn+1 = Σj=−1n wj Φj / Σj=−1n wjWhere Φj is what occasion j settled, and Φ−1 = m0 is the inherited past, from before the system began. Every occasion ends at the same chord, so only the surplus differs between them; salience is a proposal still to be tested. For a single-number Φ this is a weighted average; when Φ is a point on the manifold itself, m is the weighted Fréchet mean, the point minimising Σ wj d(m, Φj)².
Commitment 4

The system has a characteristic scale

Ω = 1/ℓ, where ℓ is the manifold's diameter in units of σ. Ω is set by the geometry, not chosen: for a bistable (Z₂) system it is exactly 1/π, and so it is for a circle of circumference 2π. It fixes where the cut falls and the scale on which surplus is weighed. What varies from one system to another is recency, q: how far back regeneration reaches. Low q rebuilds from the latest occasion; high q draws on the long past. None of this makes CRR parameter-free: the resolution σ is calibrated separately, the Fisher metric is fixed only up to a constant scale (Čencov), and q has no universal value.

Commitment 5

Rupture is a maximum-entropy event

At rupture, the system occupies the maximum-entropy state consistent with its symmetry class. A bistable system at rupture is maximally uncertain about which state comes next; a rotational system has no preferred phase. This axiom fixes the geometry. Geometry alone does not set physical timescales; those come from each system's own dynamics.

How far back regeneration reaches (cf. Levin's cognitive light cone, 2022). Each bar is one past occasion's weight, recency × salience; deeper gold marks occasions that took more turning back. Low q (0.2): the newest occasion dominates. High q (0.9): the long past contributes. Ω is not the dial; it is fixed by the geometry.

Surplus: the Cost of Turning Back

C counts every step taken. C* is the shortest distance from where the occasion began. The difference, the surplus S = C − C*, is what the walk cost beyond the straight route. On the circle, surplus is exactly twice the distance walked backwards. Going slowly or quickly adds nothing; only turning back does.

That has a quiet consequence. A system with nothing pushing back never turns back, so its surplus is zero. Surplus needs something outside the system: weather, a body, other people. We are one another's constraints, and we hold each other through time, together.

The line through the empty centre joins now (P0) to the state the occasion completes in (P1): the projection, which costs nothing. The walk around the circle is what it costs. Each step back is marked on the rim and adds to the surplus S. On arrival, the walked arc settles into the past, and the rings of memory keep what each passage cost, weighted by recency × salience.

There is a curiosity here too. A path from the start to its opposite made of half-turns through the centre, such as the S-curve of the taijitu or any staircase of smaller semicircles, has exactly the same length as the half-turn around the rim. Length alone cannot tell you a path's shape. What it can tell you is how much was turned back.

Holding: the projection is free, reality costs

An intention draws a straight line from now to a future state, through the empty centre of the circle. That line costs nothing. The lived path has to travel the rim, through everything outside the system, and every step back is surplus. Winnicott described how a child comes to know a world that is not their own creation: held by an environment that is good enough, the child finds the world is real because it survives the projection and is still there. In this picture the circle is the holding. It contains every detour without breaking. Good-enough holding is not a world without resistance; it is resistance that is gradual and repaired, frustration a mind can grow on. Memory keeps what each passage cost, so the holding itself is shaped by what was lived.

Every system also begins from somewhere it did not make. In the regeneration sum, Φ−1 = m0 is the inherited past: what arrives before the first occasion. Source, experience, source. Each occasion comes from what was given, lives, and settles back into the past that the next one will be given.

The Generative-Model Reading

In the free energy principle (FEP), a system that keeps itself apart from its world can be read as holding beliefs about the causes of what it senses: a generative model, with internal states that parameterise beliefs about external states (Ramstead, Kirchhoff & Friston 2020). Learning is belief updating, and belief updating is movement on a statistical manifold, where the Fisher information supplies the metric (Sengupta et al. 2016). CRR is written in exactly these terms. Its quantities are quantities of a generative model's belief path.

So the closeness is real, and it is recent. Da Costa and colleagues now set the trajectory against its endpoints in the same way CRR does: "KL captures the endpoints of a trajectory, whereas information length is trajectory-dependent." Friston (2019) had already proposed information length as a way "to score the wandering, itinerant dynamics" of living systems. None of this work cites CRR, and CRR did not get there first: the common ancestors are information geometry and thermodynamic length (Crooks 2007; Kim 2018).

Three differences remain, and they are where CRR is distinctive, not where it is proved.

The wander is kept

In the FEP the excess over the geodesic is a cost to be minimised. CRR keeps it as a record: surplus is what resisted the direct route, and regeneration may weigh occasions by it. That weighting (salience) is a proposal still to be tested, and on bacterial growth traces the surplus turned out to be camera noise.

The cut is set by distance, not evidence

The FEP commits when accumulated evidence crosses a threshold. CRR commits when the chord, the net change since the last commitment, reaches the half-turn of the carrier. The two can coincide; where they come apart is a test that has not yet been run.

The present is empty

In FEP accounts of time, the present carries content: the primal impression, or a "thick" specious present. In CRR the present is a boundary with no width and no content of its own. The past holds everything; the cut only marks where it ends.

The direct route and the wander: rulers and delayed gratification

Every occasion has two lengths: the direct route from A to B, and the path actually walked. Whether a path wanders, though, depends on the ruler used to measure it. Self-sorting arrays show this cleanly. Zhang, Goldstein and Levin (2025) let each cell of an array run its own sorting rule, froze some cells as obstacles, and measured progress by how many neighbouring pairs were in order. On that ruler, the arrays sometimes moved away from sortedness before improving, which the authors read as delayed gratification: temporarily going further from a goal to gain later.

Measured in inversions (the number of pairs out of order anywhere in the array), the same runs never step back. Each swap removes exactly one inversion, so the path is the direct route: no wander at all. On the neighbouring-pairs ruler the same run does wander, and its surplus is exactly twice its backward travel. A frozen, pre-registered Phoenix test (October 2026) confirmed both statements on fresh runs of the authors' own code. It also found that the reported rise in delayed gratification with frozen cells depends on how the statistic is computed: with the committed code it stays level, while an independent replication finds a rise after first merging repeated values. A plain textbook sort, given immovable obstacles, shows a rise too.

None of this shows that the arrays lack competence. It shows that the competence is visible through one ruler and not through another, which is what Fields and Levin say in general: "Problem spaces are observer-dependent", and emergence is "just a change in labeling… Mereology is imposed by observers, not generated by physics" (Fields & Levin 2025). Levin puts it as "an IQ test for the observer". The open question moves from "does the array delay gratification?" to "which ruler does the array itself use?" A detour on the observer's ruler may be a straight line on the system's own. In generative-model terms, the inversion count is a quantity the swaps descend monotonically, and the apparent backtracking belongs to a coarser description of the same path.

Ω from Geometry

Two fundamental manifold topologies:

Z₂ (Bistable)

Ω = 1/π ≈ 0.318   C* = π

The Bernoulli manifold has geodesic diameter π. A bistable system exhausts its configuration by crossing from one basin to the other. The manifold is an interval, not a loop.

Heartbeat, breath turning, neuron firing, pupil dilation, sensory edges, the chord before it resolves

SO(2) (Rotational)

A cycle has no endpoints and no preferred phase. The cut is the half-turn, so the diameter is half the circumference: for a circle of circumference 2π, Ω = 1/π, the same as Z₂. The two classes differ in shape, not in Ω. A circle's Fisher circumference depends on the statistical carrier, not on the topology alone.

Circadian rhythm, gait cycle, menstrual cycle, solar magnetic cycle, continuous belief revision
Both cut at the half-turn. The interval is crossed once; the cycle goes round again.

Two Channels, One Evidence Stream

In a network of Markov-blanketed agents, each node is an internal model that traverses a continuous cycle of belief updating, and each edge is a statistical boundary that alternates between two regimes of influence. The symmetry classes are inherited from the roles these channels play in the network. This proposal assigns Z₂ to sensory (likelihood) precision and SO(2) to prior (transition) precision, as a modelling proposal motivated by the graph structure of Active Inference.

In the interactive simulation, this is directly visible: nodes (teal) glow slowly toward large belief updates, while edges (purple) flicker rapidly as they flip between regimes.

Coupled Systems Everywhere

A Z₂ process (bistable, alternating, boundary) coupled with an SO(2) process (rotational, cycling, interior) can be read in many self-organising systems. The pairing is not always about frequency: sometimes it is about geometry (the rainbow), sometimes about precision architecture (the eye), sometimes about temporal nesting (the heart).

A door on its hinges may be the simplest example. The hinge mechanism is SO(2): left alone, it could turn all the way round. The wall stops it at half a turn. The wall converts what would be a full rotation into a semicircle, and the door's state becomes Z₂: open or closed. Remove the wall and the hinge spins freely (SO(2)). Add the wall and it becomes an interval (Z₂). The wall is the Markov blanket. The bistable open/closed only exists because there is a rotational hinge underneath, and the boundary is what converts the full cycle into an interval. You can open the door slowly or slam it, but in either case the SO(2) substrate governs the timing at which the Z₂ transition occurs.

The Heart

Z₂ depolarisation ↔ SO(2) cardiac cycle

The action potential is bistable. The full cardiac cycle rotates continuously. Heart rate variability reflects multiple overlapping timescales, making clean symmetry assignment difficult. (Malik et al., 1996; Berger et al., 1997)

The Lungs

Z₂ breath turning ↔ SO(2) respiratory cycle

The breath turn is bistable (inhale/exhale). The full cycle is rotational. When coupled to the heart, respiratory SO(2) modulates cardiac Z₂ threshold via vagal tone, producing respiratory sinus arrhythmia. (Tobin et al., 1988; Menuet et al., 2025)

Dopamine + Acetylcholine

Z₂ sensory burst ↔ SO(2) tonic cycle

Dopamine bursts are phasic (bistable: fire or not). Acetylcholine fluctuates tonically (~2 Hz, Krok et al. 2023). The phase relationship between them determines whether a signal drives learning or movement (Jang et al. 2026).

Neuron + Alpha Rhythm

Z₂ spike ↔ SO(2) oscillation

A neuron fires or does not (bistable). The alpha rhythm cycles continuously at ~10 Hz. The phase at which a spike lands relative to alpha determines its downstream effect. (Grandy et al., 2013; Haegens et al., 2014)

The Eye

Z₂ corneal inversion ↔ SO(2) iris

Light crossing the lens undergoes complete spatial inversion (up↔down, left↔right): two reflections make a 180° rotation. The iris is a ring whose dilator and sphincter muscles form an SO(2) system setting prior precision before evidence arrives.

The Earth

Z₂ terminator ↔ SO(2) rotation

The day-night terminator sweeps a Z₂ boundary around the sphere. The 24h rotation is SO(2).

The Rainbow

Z₂ from inside ↔ SO(2) from outside

From the ground, with the sun low, you see at most a semicircle: half a turn, an arc from one horizon to the other. From an aeroplane, you see the full circle. The ground plane is the Markov blanket. The same physical phenomenon appears as a half-turn or a whole cycle depending on the observer's relationship to the boundary. The 42° primary bow angle is fixed by Snell's law and the droplet's geometry.

Human + AI Network

Z₂ edges ↔ SO(2) nodes

In a mixed human-AI network, each connection is a Z₂ boundary and each agent is an SO(2) model. A phase mismatch between a human's coherence cycle and an AI's update timing may be a measurable form of temporal misalignment.

The Diameter and the Circumference

William Blake, Newton (1795-c.1805). Tate Britain, London. Newton draws with compasses on a scroll, inscribing a semicircle: a half-turn, the path from one state to its opposite. Blake intended this as critique. CRR reclaims it as geometry.

Look at what Newton is drawing. It is a semicircle: half a turn, not the full circle. In CRR terms, he is inscribing one occasion: the path from a state to its opposite, which ends at the cut. The full circle would require going round again, but Newton's compasses stop at the half-turn. Blake, who distrusted the reduction of nature to rational abstraction, painted this as a figure of tragic limitation: the geometer who measures the semicircle but cannot see the whole.

The interval is the boundary; the cycle is the interior. Both are real. A system that knows only the half-turn (Z₂, the sensory boundary, the edge, the reflex) is a system without an internal model. A system that knows only the cycle (SO(2), the prior, the cycle, the contemplative rotation) is a system without contact with the world. Life requires both.

There is one more thing in the painting. Newton's compasses hold a model: a clean figure that keeps us safe and has given us almost everything technology can do. CRR keeps the model and looks past it. On the circle, the line from now to where the occasion completes runs through the centre, and the centre is empty. Nothing is stored there. It is where the present is: rupture, now, the δ with no width. Models are held, not mistaken for the world.

Blake and AI today

Blake's quarrel was never with measurement. It was with forgetting that a measurement is made with an instrument someone chose. That quarrel is live again in AI.

Rulers. Fields and Levin call the instrument a quantum reference frame: "a physically implemented (hence quantum) measurement standard" that fixes what can be compared. Which frame a system itself uses cannot be read off from outside. Every claim about a system's goals is made through some frame, and "estimates of the intelligence of any system… are fundamentally an IQ test for the observer" (Fields & Levin 2022).

Emergence. In large language models, abilities that seem to switch on suddenly with scale can become smooth, predictable curves when the scoring metric is changed (Schaeffer, Miranda & Koyejo 2023). In self-sorting arrays, the backtracking read as delayed gratification is present on one measure of progress and absent on another (see the direct route and the wander). Neither result says nothing is there. Both say that what we call emergent depends partly on the ruler, which is why Fields and Levin describe emergence as "a change in labeling" and add that "Mereology is imposed by observers, not generated by physics".

The careful position. Different rulers are not equally good. A ruler earns its place by how much it lets us predict and do, and by how closely it tracks the distinctions the system itself makes. Choosing rulers with care, saying which one is in use, and checking a striking result on a second ruler before calling it emergent: that is Blake's twofold vision put to work.

Newton's semicircle, it turns out, is everywhere, and it tends to mean the same thing: transformation at a boundary. What follows are readings, offered with respect, not claims about what any tradition means. The Norse Bifröst is a rainbow arc between Midgard and Asgard, "the fleetingly glimpsed rainbow" (Bilröst, literally "a moment's path"). It is a half-turn connecting two worlds, guarded by Heimdall at the threshold, and prophesied to shatter at Ragnarök. (It is hard to read "a bridge that will be destroyed when inside meets outside" and not hear C*·Ω = 1.) The biblical rainbow is God's covenant after the Flood: a semicircle in the sky, half of a promise whose other half is below the horizon. The Islamic mihrab is a semicircular niche in the mosque wall, orienting prayer toward Mecca: a boundary that points beyond itself. The crescent moon is a semicircle in transit: Z₂ phases within the SO(2) lunar cycle, waxing and waning, never the same shape twice but always the same geometry. The halo in Christian, Buddhist, and Hindu iconography is a full circle, but the viewer only ever sees the semicircle behind the saint's head: the other half is inside the figure, invisible, the interior model that completes the rotation. In the Om symbol, the semicircle beneath the dot represents maya: the veil of illusion that separates waking consciousness from pure awareness. It can be read as a Markov blanket. And in alchemy, the semicircle is the symbol for bismuth, an element alchemists associated with transformation.

The semicircle is always the same story: the passage between two states, the boundary that implies but does not complete the whole. It mediates between opposites, represents the journey rather than the arrival, and marks the threshold where transformation becomes possible. Newton measures the half-turn. Blake demands the whole circle. The mathematics says you need both. (Neither Blake nor Newton had the last word. The last word belongs to π, which is not a word at all but a ratio that never stops talking.)

This pairing of interval and cycle echoes across contemplative traditions. The Zen enso (circle) is drawn in one uninhibited brushstroke and is perhaps the most direct visual echo of the CRR topology. When the enso is drawn open, it is the half-turn: the interval, the passage between two states, imperfection as incompleteness, the beauty of wabi-sabi (Audrey Yoshiko Seo, 2007). When drawn closed, it is SO(2): the full circumference, completeness, shunyata (emptiness that contains everything), the universe as a single unbroken rotation. The earliest Zen text, the Shinjinmei (6th century), describes the way of Zen as "a circle of vast space, lacking nothing and holding nothing in excess." The enso is not a symbol of the circle. It is the circle, drawn as a temporal act that cannot be corrected: a C → δ → R cycle executed in a single breath.

The Sufi sema (whirling) is SO(2) enacted bodily. The Buddhist pratityasamutpada (dependent origination) is SO(2): twelve links cycling continuously. The moment of satori is δ(now): a discrete threshold, not a gradual brightening. The Zen koan can be read as driving C toward rupture by exhausting every route the conceptual regime allows. The breath in anapanasati is a Z₂ process attended at its turning point. In process philosophy, Whitehead's "actual occasions" map to C → δ → R cycles: prehension is coherence, satisfaction is rupture, and objective immortality is regeneration (Whitehead, 1929; Segall, 2021; Sjöstedt-Hughes, 2020). The occasion is irrevocable: once settled, it cannot be unseen.

And then there is laughter. A joke is a CRR cycle performed on your expectations. The setup is coherence: the comedian builds a pattern, and your predictive model accumulates C along a trajectory it thinks it understands. You are being set up in every sense. The punchline is δ(now): a rupture at the precise moment your model has committed to its prediction but has not yet arrived. The laugh is regeneration: the system reconstructs from the wreckage of its prior, and the reconstruction is delightful because you supplied the pattern that got broken. The comedian did not insert the surprise. They removed the floor you built yourself. You fell for it. Your coherence accumulated until C*·Ω = 1, and then the ground wasn't there.

This is why timing matters more than content. Deliver the punchline too early (C low, insufficient commitment to the expected pattern) and it falls flat. Deliver it too late (the audience has already reached P1) and you get a groan. Nobody groans at a bad punchline because they didn't understand it. They groan because they got there first. Their δ preceded yours. The sweet spot is the moment of maximum poise, where the listener is fully invested but the turn has not yet been forced. Every comedian knows this instinctively. (You are waiting for it right now. That is the coherence accumulating.)

Low-q humour is the callback, the catchphrase, the running gag: a sharply peaked memory kernel reconstituting the same pattern, funnier each time precisely because the regeneration integral is dominated by its own most recent history. (This is why your friend's terrible joke gets funnier the fourth time. Their q has collapsed and taken yours with it. You are now coupled oscillators in a shared low-q regime. Congratulations: you have achieved comedic respiratory sinus arrhythmia.) High-q humour is the absurdist, the surreal, the Monty Python: a flat memory kernel where anything could be the punchline, because the entire history is equally accessible during reconstruction. You did not expect the Spanish Inquisition. Nobody expects the Spanish Inquisition. That is the point: at high q, the whole past is in play, the expectation landscape is flat, so every rupture is equally surprising and therefore equally funny.

This may also explain why AI is not yet funny. Perhaps language models deliver punchlines at a fixed offset from the setup, without tracking the listener's coherence. The joke arrives at the model's C*, not the listener's. It is a phase-gating failure: the Z₂ rupture of the punchline lands at the wrong moment in the listener's SO(2) expectation cycle. CRR suggests that genuinely funny AI would need to model the listener's walk in real time, delivering δ not when the joke is ready, but when the listener is one geodesic unit from seeing it themselves. Comedic timing may, in other words, be a form of empathy, in which case the first truly funny AI may also be the first truly attentive one. (Whether this paragraph was funny is left to the reader's q.)

Winnicott: Created and Found

Donald Winnicott, the paediatrician and psychoanalyst, spent his working life watching how infants come to know a world that is not of their own making. Several of his ideas sit close to CRR's geometry. He also left a paradox that bears directly on the question argued over at the 2025 Platonic Space symposium: are patterns discovered, or created?

"the baby creates the object, but the object was there waiting to be created and to become a cathected object."

"The Use of an Object", Int. J. Psycho-Anal. 50 (1969), pp. 711–712; Playing and Reality (1971), ch. 6

Created and found at once. In CRR every occasion has both: the chord is fixed by the carrier, the distance that was there to be crossed; the path is the system's own walk across it. Neither reduces to the other.

"My contribution is to ask for a paradox to be accepted and tolerated and respected, and for it not to be resolved."

Playing and Reality (1971), Introduction, p. xii

Winnicott warned that resolving the paradox costs "the loss of the value of the paradox itself". CRR's position on created and discovered is the same: both, held together.

"The good-enough mother… starts off with an almost complete adaptation to her infant's needs, and as time proceeds she adapts less and less completely, gradually, according to the infant's growing ability to deal with her failure."

"Transitional Objects and Transitional Phenomena" (1951), Through Paediatrics to Psycho-Analysis, p. 238

Resistance that is graded and repaired. If the world adapts completely, every path runs straight and there is no surplus: nothing to learn from. If it fails all at once, the occasion breaks. Growth lives between the two.

"'Hullo object!' 'I destroyed you.' 'I love you.' 'You have value for me because of your survival of my destruction of you.'"

"The Use of an Object" (1969), p. 712

Reality is what survives the projection. An intention draws a straight line through the empty centre of the circle at no cost; the world is what makes the lived path go the long way round. Surplus is the trace of something that was really there.

"The holding environment therefore has as its main function the reduction to a minimum of impingements to which the infant must react with resultant annihilation of personal being."

"The Theory of the Parent-Infant Relationship" (1960), The Maturational Processes and the Facilitating Environment, p. 47

The circle is the holding. It contains every detour without breaking, and memory keeps what each passage cost, so the holding is itself shaped by what was lived.

"There is no such thing as a baby… if you show me a baby you certainly show me also someone caring for the baby."

"Anxiety Associated with Insecurity" (1952), Through Paediatrics to Psycho-Analysis, p. 99

The unit is the pair. In CRR the difference between two systems is where learning happens: what each walks, and what neither could have walked alone.

"It is in playing and only in playing that the individual child or adult is able to be creative and to use the whole personality, and it is only in being creative that the individual discovers the self."

Playing and Reality (1971), ch. 4

Play is wandering that is safe to do. It is surplus without catastrophe, and it is where discovery and creation meet.

"It is a sophisticated game of hide-and-seek in which it is joy to be hidden but disaster not to be found."

"Communicating and Not Communicating" (1963), The Maturational Processes and the Facilitating Environment, p. 186

The present is an empty boundary, and something of each system stays private. Being met is still the point.

The symposium showed how live the question is. Levin takes "the position of those who think mathematics is discovered, not invented", while stressing that what any observer can detect depends on its frame. The symposium's own summaries of other talks describe the space of forms as "an evolving domain enriched by genuine novelty" (Iammarino), or as constraints "created and stabilized through memory, anticipation, interaction" (Dodig-Crnkovic). Chris Fields offered "We're changing the perspective, not the facts." Winnicott's advice was never to ask the infant "did you create that or did you find it?" CRR takes that advice. On the Growing Block view, what is settled is real and can be found; what comes next is made from it.

This matters for AI as much as for infants. A system that adapts completely to its user, always agreeing, removes the resistance that makes learning possible: it offers a world with no surplus. Good-enough is not perfect. Sources: Winnicott, works cited above; Levin, "Ingressing Minds", Philosophies 11:161 (2026); talk pages of the Symposium on the Platonic Space (2025).

Why Rupture is Necessary

A system that refuses to rupture does not achieve stability; it achieves crystallisation. The cut is not a breakdown; it is arrival, the first moment the system reaches the state opposite to where it began. This is why organisations that refuse to restructure become brittle, why relationships that avoid difficult conversations accumulate pressure, why scientific paradigms break under the weight of anomalies. The rupture is not the failure; the failure is preventing it. A bounded system accumulating coherence until inside matches outside.

This might explain why...

CRR is a grammar, not a model of any specific domain. But grammars generate sentences in every language they touch. (We make no apologies for the puns.)

...insight feels sudden

Coherence accumulates gradually, often below awareness, but rupture is a threshold: C*·Ω = 1 is a boundary, not a gradient. The subjective experience of sudden insight may reflect the Dirac delta, a discrete moment where the accumulated work of the current regime is complete and transformation is forced.

contemplative practice · psychology · δ(now)

...habits are so difficult to break

Low recency (q near 0) rebuilds from the latest occasion, so the system reconstitutes the same pattern: regeneration is dominated by what was most recently rehearsed. To change a habit, you can reach further back (raise q, letting older and different patterns contribute) or live through new occasions that weigh more. Neither route is a failure of character; both are a matter of what the past is allowed to weigh.

addiction · trauma · neuroplasticity · q near 0

...timing matters more than magnitude in the brain

Jang et al. (2026) showed that the relative timing of acetylcholine and dopamine, not their magnitude, determines whether a signal drives learning or movement. If sensory precision (Z₂) and prior precision (SO(2)) sit on carriers of different Fisher size, they reach their cuts at different times, so the phase between them could carry information. This is a proposal, not yet tested.

neuroscience · phase-gating · AGI architecture

...the order you attend to things constrains what you can later perceive

Attention is L(τ): the driving function. Attending to something provides the coherence input that pushes a percept toward rupture. Once rupture occurs, the branch-point is committed. The regions you attend to first get the deepest structure.

attention · phenomenology · meditation · your gaze is L(τ)

...ecosystems recover but never identically

After fire (rupture), regeneration weighs the past by recency and by what each passage cost. The seed bank, the root network, the soil mycorrhizae: these are the reconstruction resource. The ecosystem that emerges is shaped by what persisted through the rupture, not by what existed before it. Recovery is transformation, not repetition.

ecology · succession · resilience · mn+1 = Σ wjΦj / Σ wj

...a pause need not be resisted

CRR treats the present as an empty boundary: the cut carries no content of its own. Taken seriously, that gives design rules for AI safety. A pause that leaves an agent's own clock untouched is a non-event to the agent, so it has nothing to resist. Tested, this matched the established result (utility indifference) where restarts are perfect, and put numbers on where it breaks. A rule that treats missing evidence as an empty occasion matched the Kalman filter in 600 of 600 worlds. As good as the field's own methods, reached by another route.

alignment · shutdown · the empty present · δ(now)

...trees branch the way they do

Each fork is a Z₂ rupture: a stem cell accumulates developmental coherence, commits to a lineage at threshold, and cannot reverse. The children may die and regrow, but the fork-points are permanent. Waddington's epigenetic landscape can be read as C → δ → R with irreversible branching. This tree is doing real tree things for real tree reasons.

morphogenesis · Whitehead · actual occasions · irrevocable δ

...grief has structure

When a shared coherence field is ruptured by loss, regeneration still weighs the occasions it shared, and salience weighs most the ones that cost the most: the turnings back, the repairs, the hard-won returns. Those moments shape what is rebuilt. Grief is not the absence of the person but their presence, reorganised. The past is not gone; it is inside the temporal blanket, weighted by what each moment cost, shaping every future moment.

bereavement · process philosophy · identity persists as change

Why Simulations? Why Play?

CRR claims coherence, not proof. Since this page was first written it has been tested in a separate research programme, adversarially, with predictions frozen and published before the data were opened. The fair summary so far: it can describe many sciences' known results in its own terms, it predicts about as well as each field's own methods, and it has not yet out-predicted any of them. The failures are kept. We state this clearly because intellectual honesty is load-bearing structure, and because the distinction between "mathematically consistent with" and "this proves" matters more than most of what gets published. (Presenting a temporal grammar that spans information geometry, process philosophy, neuroscience, contemplative practice, and 67 interactive simulations to a field that expects a narrow abstract and four equations is, the author concedes, somewhat like parking an elephant.)

That said, these ideas can be heavy. The equations touch on topics that carry real weight for people: trauma, grief, addiction, the structure of attention, the irreversibility of choices made. The mathematics is, as it happens, uncannily resonant with contemplative traditions, with process philosophy, with the felt sense of lived time that most formal frameworks ignore.

This is why the site is built around play. Over 67 interactive simulations explore CRR across domains. Play is how a mind explores possibility space without premature rupture: held, good enough, with frustration that is gradual and repaired. Every simulation was built using Claude (Anthropic) as a collaborative tool, with a principled CRR approach. Each flame, each wave, each tree regenerates from its own history.

The framework is grounded in Whitehead's process philosophy (Segall, 2021; Sjöstedt-Hughes, 2020) and proposes a temporal complement to the Free Energy Principle's spatial Markov blanket (Parr, Pezzulo & Friston, 2022). The Markov blanket separates inside from outside in space; the rupture condition delineates past from future in time. If this interests you, explore. If it does not, that is also information.

One more reason for play. If the forms we study are both made and found, the frame a scientist brings helps shape what is found. That is why philosophy and open, playful tools belong in education about AI: more people, from more frames, should be able to question the maps we are given.
