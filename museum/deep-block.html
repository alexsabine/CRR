Five readings

Added on 24 November 2026, when this piece was put back. These are ways of reading the three rooms. None of them is a finding, and nothing in the rooms is evidence about the world.

1. Enduring and inheriting

Room one. Two lanterns keep the same uneven rhythm. In one, something lasts from blink to blink. The other ends at every blink and leaves a note, and the next blink is worked out from the notes. Watching cannot tell you which is which. A self that lasts and a self that is remade each moment from its own record would look the same from outside.

2. The record and the pen

Room one again. The forger's pen changes only the lantern that runs on its record. The ember lantern does not care what is written about it. A thing that steers by its own record is only as sound as the record. When people do the forging, to hit a target, this goes by the name of Goodhart's law.

3. Growing block and block

Room two. The corridor laid slab by slab under your feet, and the same corridor made all at once, have the same fingerprint to the last digit. From inside the walk you cannot tell whether the future is being made or was already there.

4. Made and found

Room two, from the walker's side. Each slab is made by your step. The whole was there to be found. Both are true of the same stones. Winnicott said of a child's first treasured thing that we agree never to ask whether it was made or found. Blake has it from the other side: “Eternity is in love with the productions of time.”

5. What did not insist

At every join in the corridor stands a frame with a lamp, and in the frame there is nothing. The museum's owner reads that nothing as a giving-way: something that does not insist on being, so that the next slab can be. In his words, “It is not, so everything else can be.”

This last is a position, not a result. Room one shows why nothing here could count against it. It has its own book on the metaphysics shelf of the library, What did not insist, marked untestable.

Winnicott, Playing and Reality (1971), from memory. Blake, The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, plate 7.
