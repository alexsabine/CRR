About this table

An edge, in this museum's words, is the place where what you already think meets something that does not fit. This is a working model of that sentence. It is small and everything in it is computed.

The model

The territory is one number at each place, a height h(x, y), made from a seed so it is the same every visit. The maps use a coarse grid of 44 by 30 cells. Heights are shown on a scale where the table runs from about 0 to 100.

Alone. A map-maker walks a round patch. At every step it reads the cells within a step and a half of its feet. A reading is the true height, bent by the map-maker's own lens (a smooth error that is its alone and that it cannot see), plus mist (random noise). For each cell it keeps an estimate and a confidence, updated by the one-number Kalman rule: new estimate = old + (reading − old) × (confidence of the reading ÷ total confidence). Unwalked paper stays blank and is scored as a guess of level ground.

Choose a map-maker and the caption gives two numbers: how far its map is from the table, and how far it reckons it is. The second comes only from its own confidence and shrinks with every reading. The lens never shows up in it. From inside one map there is nothing to check the map against. The board called Drift makes the same point.

Meeting at an edge. Where two map-makers have both walked a cell, they compare. If they agree to within 1.2, nothing happens. If not, each crosses 6% of its share of the gap per step, and its share is the other's confidence over the sum: precision-weighted averaging. With equal confidence they end midway. What is learned is kept as a correction to that cell, and confidence is not raised by it, so the same evidence is not counted twice. The pink seam shows the gap, cell by cell, and fades as it closes.

Single vision makes one map-maker's confidence in its own readings absolute. It drops whatever it had taken from others, stops revising what it has drawn, and at its edges its share of every gap is nothing, so the others cross the whole way. Two lenses averaged are usually nearer the truth than one, so this usually raises the error on the ground they share, though not always. Switching it off does not undo it: by then the others agree with it, and where maps agree nothing happens. Blake's prayer was May God us keep / From Single vision & Newtons sleep.

The stranger arrives sure of a map made on a different table (another seed). Its seams are the widest. It learns most there, and its neighbours are pulled furthest from the truth.

The readouts. Error is the root-mean-square distance from the true heights over the whole table, blank paper included. The pooled map takes, in each cell, the confidence-weighted average of every map that has walked it. Learning is counted as error removed (squared, summed over all maps), and each update is booked to its cause: first sight of a cell, re-reading it, or a seam. The share shown is seams ÷ (seams + re-reading); the smaller figure counts first sight as well. It is whatever the run gives, and it can be negative when seams have taught more wrong than right.

When pooling helps. Once seams are quiet, the pooled map equals the best single map inside that map's patch and adds the others' ground outside it. So pooled error is no worse than the best single error provided nobody's confidence is absolute and each added map is nearer the truth than blank paper. A stranger or one single vision can break that, and the numbers will show it.

Sources

Frith, “A Duet for one”, Consciousness and Cognition 36 (2015), where two agents each weight the other's signal by its precision and come into step. This table borrows only that weighting. It is not their model, which is about two birds predicting each other's song. W. Blake, letter to Thomas Butts, 22 November 1802.

What this leaves out

Nearly everything about people. A height is one number, and what people know is not. Here there is a true table to score against, and outside this room nobody is handed one. The stranger's map is true of somewhere else, and on this table that can only show up as error. Map-makers here never refuse to meet, never mishear, and always report their confidence honestly unless you switch single vision on. Nobody generalises: a correction learned at a seam stays in that cell. Whether minds weight each other by precision in this way is a modelling proposal and not a settled finding.
