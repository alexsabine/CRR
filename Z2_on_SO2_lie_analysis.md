# CRR Z₂-on-SO(2) Lie Analysis (Iteration 1)

**Question.** Are CRR's Z₂ ruptures cuts or projections on a Lie-group structure carrying
the SO(2) continual manifold? If so, what is the formal statement, what does it predict,
and what 2026 peer-reviewed work supports or undermines it?

**Stance.** This document treats the user's framing as a working axiom — *Z₂ ruptures
happen on the SO(2) continual manifold* — and tests it against the canonical CRR
formalism, the multiscale and meta-theorem proof sketches, and against 2026 literature.

## 1. The Lie-group skeleton beneath CRR's two symmetry classes

### 1.1 Setup

CRR (`README.md`, lines 41–51; `CRR_COMPREHENSIVE_SUMMARY.md` Part II) commits to two
fundamental Fisher–Rao manifolds:

| Class | Manifold | Geodesic length ℓ | Ω = 1/ℓ | Predicted CV = Ω/2 |
|-------|----------|-------------------|---------|--------------------|
| Z₂    | Bernoulli arc, parameter p ∈ [0,1] | π    | 1/π     | 1/(2π) ≈ 0.159 |
| SO(2) | Circle S¹ ≅ U(1)                   | 2π   | 1/(2π)  | 1/(4π) ≈ 0.0796 |

The ratio CV(Z₂) : CV(SO(2)) = 2 : 1 is asserted as a **topological invariant**.

### 1.2 The Lie-theoretic identity that makes this exact

The claim "Z₂ ruptures on the SO(2) continuous manifold" is mathematically the assertion
that there is a short exact sequence of Lie groups

$$
1 \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{Z}_2 \;\xrightarrow{\;\iota\;}\; SO(2) \;\xrightarrow{\;p\;}\; SO(2)/\mathbb{Z}_2 \;\longrightarrow\; 1
$$

where:

* **ι** embeds the Z₂ subgroup as `{0, π}` ⊂ S¹, i.e. the antipodal pair `{1, −1}` ⊂ U(1).
* **p** is the 2:1 covering homomorphism `θ ↦ 2θ mod 2π`. Its image is again
  isomorphic to SO(2) — special to dim 1 because every connected compact 1-D Lie
  group is U(1).
* The deck-transformation group of `p` is exactly Z₂.

Equivalent presentations:

* As principal bundles: `SO(2) → SO(2)/Z₂` is a principal Z₂-bundle.
* As covering spaces: `SO(2) → RP¹ ≅ S¹` is the orientation double cover of the real
  projective line.
* As Lie algebras: `𝔰𝔬(2) ≅ ℝ` is unchanged; only the global identification changes.
  The kernel ker(p) is the discrete subgroup generated by exp(π·X) where X spans
  𝔰𝔬(2).

### 1.3 Cut vs. projection — the same operation, two languages

A **cut** view: take SO(2), choose a fundamental domain of the Z₂ action — any half-arc
of length π — and glue endpoints. The Bernoulli manifold `[0,1]` with Fisher–Rao metric
*is* this fundamental domain (length π).

A **projection** view: take SO(2), apply the covering map `p`, and identify antipodes.
The resulting quotient is again S¹ but with all natural lengths halved.

These are dual. A Z₂ rupture on the cover (SO(2)) is the same event as a return-to-base
on the quotient (Bernoulli/RP¹). The factor 2 in `ℓ_{SO(2)}/ℓ_{Z₂}` is the **covering
degree**, an integer topological invariant that no parameter choice can perturb.

This locks the CRR ratio CV(Z₂)/CV(SO(2)) = 2 to a property of the bundle, not of any
biology. It is the strongest available robustness statement for CRR's two-class
prediction.

## 3. Proof checks

Five independent checks, in order of confidence.

### 3.1 ✅ Covering-degree check (passes)

`p: SO(2) → SO(2)/Z₂` has degree 2. Therefore for any push-forward measure on the
quotient, the lifted measure on the cover has twice the total natural length per cycle.
This forces `ℓ_{SO(2)} = 2 · ℓ_{Z₂}` and hence `Ω_{Z₂}/Ω_{SO(2)} = 2` exactly.
Cross-check: `1/π ÷ 1/(2π) = 2`. ✓

### 3.2 ✅ Bonnet–Myers consistency (passes, conditional)

`crr_first_principles_proofs.md` §2.3 invokes Bonnet–Myers: positive Ricci ≥ κ implies
diameter ≤ π/√κ. For Bernoulli with constant Ricci 1, diameter = π. ✓
The Wikipedia/Scholarpedia entries on the Fisher–Rao metric corroborate that the
Bernoulli statistical manifold has diameter exactly π under Fisher–Rao. The Z₂ value of
ℓ = π is therefore forced, not chosen.
Status: **passes for the Z₂ class**. The SO(2) class is not Bonnet–Myers but
holonomy-of-U(1); see §3.3.

### 3.3 ✅ Holonomy check for SO(2) (passes)

The U(1) Wilson loop has full period 2π. CRR's `Ω_{SO(2)} = 1/(2π)` follows from
identifying coherence with holonomy phase. This is the gauge-theory derivation already
sketched in §9 of the first-principles proofs, and is consistent with the Bohr–Sommerfeld
phase-space quantisation (`CRR_PHYSICS_ANALYSIS_COMPREHENSIVE.md` §6.4).

### 3.4 ⚠️ CV = Ω/2 derivation (partial, not fully rigorous)

The argument: at rupture, place the Dirac unit mass symmetrically on the past/future
boundary; this is Bernoulli(½), giving variance 1/4 and σ = 1/2. Then
CV = σ/E[C\*] = (1/2)/(1/Ω) = Ω/2.

**Gap:** the assertion `σ(C\*) = 1/2` mixes two units of measurement. The Bernoulli
variance `p(1−p) = 1/4` is dimensionless in the boundary-coordinate (mass fraction).
To obtain `σ(C\*)` in coherence-units one would need to multiply by some natural
boundary-scale; the derivation tacitly takes this scale to be 1 nat. That is an extra
commitment, not a theorem.

The repo itself flags this in `CRR_COMPREHENSIVE_SUMMARY.md` Part X: *"the 1/2 is the
standard deviation of a Bernoulli(½) variable…well-motivated…but is itself a commitment
rather than a theorem derived from measure theory alone."*

**However:** the *ratio* CV(Z₂)/CV(SO(2)) is independent of this commitment. Whatever
the absolute σ(C\*) is, it is the same on both sides of the bundle (the cover and the
base see the same Dirac at rupture). So the ratio of 2 survives even if the absolute
constant is wrong. The Lie-group analysis therefore protects the *ratio* claim much more
strongly than the *absolute* claim.

This is the most important finding of this iteration: **CRR's topological prediction
(ratio = 2) is on much firmer ground than its absolute prediction (CV = Ω/2).** That
should be reflected in how CRR is communicated to reviewers.

### 3.5 ⚠️ Multiscale Ω-π scaling (open)

`multiscale_crr_proof_sketch.md` §8 conjectures `Ω^{(n+1)} = π · Ω^{(n)}`. The Lie
analysis above does *not* derive this; it only fixes the within-class ratio of 2.
A scale-to-scale factor of π would correspond to a *sequence* of Z₂ → SO(2) → Spin(2) →
… coverings, but in 1D this sequence collapses (every connected compact 1-D Lie group is
U(1)). The conjecture therefore needs a different mechanism — most likely a bounded Kan
extension argument (`CRR_Bounded_Kan_Extension_QED_v2.pdf`) — and remains the principal
unproven structural claim.

### 4.2 What this 2026 literature is missing

These are the gaps a referee should hold the literature to, as identified by reading
the supporting papers against the CRR framework on the Lie-group side:

1. **No covering-degree derivation of the 2:1 ratio.** Tucker–Luu–Friston discuss
   excitatory/inhibitory balance qualitatively but do not assign these channels to
   distinct Lie-group symmetry classes. The factor 2 between them is empirically
   suggestive (E/I, REM/NREM, dorsal/ventral) but is not derived from the
   `SO(2) → SO(2)/Z₂` covering. CRR supplies the derivation; the FEP literature has
   the data and lacks the geometry.

2. **No first-passage geometry on the Lie group itself.** Stochastic-Fisher-information
   work (Liu, Ito–Dechant 2025) treats Brownian particles in Euclidean state spaces. It
   does not transport the Cramér–Rao saturation onto compact Lie-group state spaces
   (where the diameter is bounded and threshold-crossing is generic). CRR's rupture is
   precisely a first-passage event on a compact Lie group. The 2025 papers do not
   provide closed forms for this case.

3. **No explicit Z₂ structure on the Markov blanket.** The Markov-blanket literature
   (Friston 2019; Markov-Blankets-of-Life and the 2024 Mirror Symmetries paper)
   acknowledges symmetry but does not single out Z₂ as the *blanket* symmetry while SO(2)
   is the *internal-state* symmetry. CRR's claim that the blanket is intrinsically
   bistable (Z₂) and the interior intrinsically rotational (SO(2)) is novel and untested
   by that literature.

4. **No phase-gating prediction from geometry.** Jang et al. (2026) report timing-gates
   experimentally but do not predict the specific 2:1 modal-update ratio that CRR
   derives. A pre-registered re-analysis of the Jang dataset under CRR's phase-gating
   model would be high-value.

5. **No falsifiable handle on Ω = 1/π specifically.** The Bonnet–Myers diameter argument
   gives `ℓ ≤ π/√κ`, not `ℓ = π`. A first-principles derivation of κ = 1 (constant
   sectional curvature on the Bernoulli arc) is in the standard Fisher-Rao literature,
   but the 2026 information-geometry update (MDPI Entropy 28(4), 374, "Approach to
   Fisher-Rao Metric for Infinite Dimensional Non-Parametric Information Geometry")
   addresses the *infinite-dimensional* case where the metric loses a bounded inverse —
   and so cannot ratify the finite-dimensional `Ω = 1/π` for free. CRR's `Ω = 1/π` is
   therefore *consistent with* but *not derivable from* current 2026 information
   geometry.

6. **No engagement with multi-scale Ω-π scaling.** The 2026 literature is silent on
   whether Ω scales as `π · Ω^{(n−1)}` between observation scales. This is the deepest
   open conjecture (`multiscale_crr_proof_sketch.md` §8.3).

## 5. Robust statement for the next iteration

Combining §3 and §4:

> **CRR's strongest claim is the Lie-theoretic claim.** The CV ratio of 2 between Z₂
> and SO(2) systems is the covering degree of the principal Z₂-bundle
> `SO(2) → SO(2)/Z₂`. It is a topological invariant, parameter-free, and resistant to
> any redefinition of σ(C\*).
>
> **CRR's weakest claim is the absolute CV value.** `CV = Ω/2` requires the additional
> commitment that the Dirac unit-mass jitter is exactly 1/2 nat in absolute coherence
> units. The 2026 stochastic-Fisher-information literature can in principle derive this
> from a single-trajectory speed limit, but has not yet been adapted to the compact
> Lie-group case CRR needs.
>
> **The 2026 literature converges with CRR but lacks its geometry.** Tucker–Luu–Friston
> on criticality, Mounier–Parr–Friston on flatness, and Jang et al. on phase-gated
> dopamine all produce findings structurally compatible with CRR but derived without
> the covering-degree machinery. CRR's contribution is to *predict* what these papers
> *measure*.
