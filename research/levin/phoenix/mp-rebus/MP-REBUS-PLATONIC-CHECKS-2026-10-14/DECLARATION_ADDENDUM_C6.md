# Declaration addendum: C6, the full sorting battery with the authors' own rules and measures (2026-10-14; before any run of `checks/c6_sorting_battery.py`)

**Source of the rules.** The authors' public repository `github.com/Zhangtaining/cell_research` (commit 1fd2bd5, 2024-10-30; no licence file) was cloned into the session's untrusted scratchpad and **read, not executed, and not copied into this repository**. The rules and measures below are reimplemented from that reading; the code's own names are cited so a reader can check. Where the paper's text (as reported by today's sweep) and the code differ, the code is followed and the difference is noted.

**The cell rules (from `modules/multithread/*.py`).**
- *Bubble cell* (`BubbleSortCell.move`): on each move the cell picks left or right at random (`random.random() < 0.5`), and swaps with that neighbour if they are out of order (`should_move_to`: `value > neighbour` when checking right, `value < neighbour` when checking left). A frozen cell never initiates (`swap` returns at once when `self.status == FREEZE`) but **can be swapped by an active neighbour** (the target may be `ACTIVE` or `FREEZE`). So the paper's frozen cells in the bubble experiments are "movable".
- *Insertion cell* (`InsertionSortCell`): moves only if the prefix to its left is sorted (`is_enable_to_move`, with the comparison reset at a frozen cell), and then swaps left if smaller than its left neighbour; the target may be frozen (movable).
- *Selection cell* (`SelectionSortCell`): keeps an "ideal position" that starts at the left boundary; each move compares with the occupant of the ideal position: if the occupant is frozen, the ideal advances by one (and a swap is made if the cell is smaller); if `value >= occupant` the ideal advances by one; else the cell swaps into the ideal position (a non-adjacent swap, the occupant taking the cell's old place). The cell stops when it sits at its ideal position.
- *Classical versions*: textbook bubble (left-to-right passes), insertion, selection, recording the same snapshots after each swap.

**The measures (from `analysis/`).**
- *Monotonicity error* (`delay_gratification_analysis.get_monotonicity`): the number of adjacent descents, `#{i : a[i] < a[i−1]}`. (The other `utils.get_monotonicity` returns 100 × the fraction of non-descents; the DG script uses the descent count.)
- *Delayed gratification* (`avg_wandering_range`): take the error trace after each swap; drop consecutive repeats; sum consecutive same-sign differences into runs; from the first positive run (an error increase) onward, pair each increase `d_i > 0` with the following decrease `d_j < 0` and score `(−d_j − d_i)/d_i`, the net improvement beyond the increase relative to the increase; DG is the mean over such pairs (0 if none). Reimplemented from that reading.
- *Spearman distance* (`utils.get_spearman_distance`): `Σ_i |a[i] − i|`, the footrule.
- *Aggregation* (the paper's description, as reported by the sweep: "percentage of cells with directly adjacent neighboring cells that were all the same Algotype"): the fraction of interior cells whose two neighbours share its algotype. The code's `get_aggregation_score` (sum of the longest same-type runs) is a different statistic; the description is used here and labelled.
- *CRR rulers*: inversions (global order), adjacent descents (local order, = the monotonicity error), footrule; on each, `C = Σ|Δe|`, `C* = |e_T − e_0|`, `S = C − C*`, `B = S/2`.

**Design.** n = 100 cells, values 0..99, 50 replicates per arm (seed per replicate), frozen cells 0–3 placed at random (`cells[randint].set_cell_to_freeze()`, as in `multithread_cell_sorting_with_frozen_steps.py`). Arms: classical and cell-view × bubble, insertion, selection. Chimeras (50/50 random assignment): bubble–selection, bubble–insertion, selection–insertion, and the negative control (bubble cells carrying two labels). Cross-purposes: bubble ascending with selection descending. Two CRR-specific checks: state-sufficiency and per-cell clocks.

**Predictions, fixed before the run.**
1. **Inversion carrier: `S = 0` in every replicate of every arm and frozen count.** Reason, stated as a theorem: a swap that moves the smaller of two values to the left changes the inversion count by `−(2k + 1)`, where `k` is the number of values strictly between them in value that lie between them in position; every swap in all six rules is of this kind, so no swap ever increases inversions.
2. **Footrule carrier: `S = 0` in every replicate of every arm.** Reason: for `v < o` swapped from positions `q > p` to `p, q`, the footrule change is `g(v) − g(o)` with `g(x) = |p − x| − |q − x|` nondecreasing in `x`, hence `≤ 0`.
3. **Adjacent-descent carrier: `S > 0` in at least 90 % of replicates for every arm** (a swap at `(i, i+1)` can unsettle `(i−1, i)` and `(i+1, i+2)`; a selection jump can unsettle four pairs).
4. **The authors' DG, on their measure:** positive for every arm; cell-view bubble DG rises with frozen count 0 → 3 (their 0.24 → 0.37); cell-view minus classical: bubble positive, insertion near zero, selection negative (their +0.16, +0.03 n.s., −2.77). This is the one prediction that could reproduce their result, and it is made on their measure only.
5. **Swap counts:** cell-view selection ≥ 5 × classical selection swaps (their 11 ×); cell-view bubble within 5 % of classical bubble (their z = 0.73, n.s.).
6. **Chimeras:** every mixed array sorts fully; the bubble–selection chimera's peak aggregation exceeds the negative control's peak by > 0.05 (their 0.72 against 0.61), and the control's peak stays within 0.05 of its random-label baseline (0.25 in this measure); cross-purposes ends at an intermediate sortedness between 30 % and 70 % of pairs in ascending order (their 42.5).
7. **State-sufficiency (CRR M4 as path dependence):** two arrays in the identical state whose cells carry different pasts (per-cell swap counts from a real route against counts of zero) have the same distribution of remaining swaps under the authors' rules: a two-sample permutation test on the remaining-swap count gives `p > 0.05` in at least 90 % of 40 trials. Must-fail arm: a "fatigue" variant in which a cell's chance to act decays with its own past swaps (`p_act = 1/(1 + 0.05 × swaps)`) gives `p < 0.05` in at least 80 % of trials. Meaning: the sorting worlds are state-sufficient (PR2's world S); any "memory" the authors read into them lives in the array state, so CRR's path-dependence commitment has nothing to grip here, and the paper's "no feedback, no stochasticity, no memory" is correct about the dynamics.
8. **Per-cell clocks (CRR's two clocks):** in bubble and insertion, each cell's own count of swaps experienced equals its inversion degree in the initial permutation exactly (Spearman 1.000, max absolute difference 0), for classical and cell-view alike; in selection it does not (Spearman < 0.95). Meaning: in the adjacent-swap algorithms a cell's whole history of becoming is fixed by the initial arrangement, so there is nothing for the cell's "patience" to be a strategy about.

**Meaning of the outcomes.** 1–3 hold: the carrier-relativity of "delayed gratification" is a theorem about the rules, not a property of the cells (DESCR). 4 holds: the paper's DG numbers are reproduced on its own measure, and they are the local shadow of global monotone progress. 4 fails: the reimplementation differs from theirs in a way not captured by reading the code, and nothing about their numbers is claimed. 7 and 8: CRR-specific readings that the paper does not run; they bound what any "history" claim about these systems could mean.
