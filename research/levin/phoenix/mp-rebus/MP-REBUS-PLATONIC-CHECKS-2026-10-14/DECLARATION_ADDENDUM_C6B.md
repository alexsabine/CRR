# Declaration addendum: C6b, state-sufficiency re-tested on a statistic that can fail (2026-10-14, 13:35Z; after reading C6, before any run of `checks/c6b_state_sufficiency_visits.py`)

**Why.** C6 prediction 7 used "remaining swaps to completion" as the statistic. For adjacent-swap-of-out-of-order-pairs dynamics the remaining swap count equals the inversion count of the state (the theorem behind prediction 1), so it is fixed by the state whatever the cells' pasts, for the authors' rules **and for the fatigue must-fail arm alike**. The must-fail arm therefore could not fail (C6: 2.5 % of trials below 0.05), and C6's prediction 7 is VOID by construction, not a result about the sorting worlds. This addendum declares a statistic that history can move.

**Statistic.** Remaining *visits* (cell activations, swapping or not) until the run halts, under the same two-route design as C6: route A reaches a state after 600 visits with real per-cell swap counts; from that identical state, 20 continuations carrying the real counts against 20 carrying zero counts; two-sample permutation test on remaining visits; 40 trials; fatigue arm `p_act = 1/(1 + 0.05 × swaps)`. Remaining swaps is recorded alongside as the known-invariant control.

**Predictions.** (a) Authors' rules: `p > 0.05` in at least 90 % of trials on remaining visits (the state is sufficient; the counters do nothing). (b) Fatigue must-fail: `p < 0.05` in at least 80 % of trials on remaining visits (the carried counts slow the cells that have swapped most). (c) Remaining swaps: `p > 0.05` in at least 90 % of trials in both arms (the invariant). If (b) fails, the fatigue strength is too weak for this test and the test is reported as under-powered, not as evidence of state-sufficiency.
