### What the differentiation shows

1. **The owner's reading is a definite position** and it is not any of the existing ten. Its signature is the conjunction: occasions (Whitehead) *without* eternal objects; precedence (Smolin) applied to forms of finite systems; distinction as the condition of relation (Spencer-Brown) *with* a metric; symmetry-breaking contingency (Curie) read from the inside; and a Growing-Block future. No single source holds all of these.
2. **Every clause, taken separately, has a mainstream home**, and the checks (Part II) show that the formal content of each is known mathematics. The novelty, if there is one, is the conjunction and the grammar, not any part.
3. **The reading's nearest formal relative with a prediction is Smolin's precedence.** M7 should either adopt that discriminator (entropy of realised forms versus precedent count, in a system where precedents are countable) or accept NOT DECIDABLE against a learning-library version of ingression.
4. **Two decisions are owed by the owner**, because the text underdetermines them and the consequences differ: (a) whether "choices at every Now" produce only the *realised* form (SSB; law-given possibilities) or also the *set* of possible forms (Peirce; evolving law); (b) whether "why these forms" is unanswerable *in principle* (Brouwer: free choice, no law behind it) or unanswerable *from law* but answerable *from history* (F5: the answer exists as record). The checks support (b) and the owner's sentence "I cannot answer that, but that is the point" is consistent with either; the Egg's M4/M5 favour (b).
5. **"Separate and together" is the best-grounded clause.** It is M6, it is Spencer-Brown's first act, it is Brouwer's two-ity, and F3 gives it an information-theoretic reading that does not depend on anything contested: without difference there is no relation, without a shared carrier there is no comparison, and the Fisher distance measures the first on the second.

### Reading the counts honestly

1. **CRR is short to state and long to use.** Six labelled statements and 1 755 words put it among the compact frameworks for *statement*, and the five-sentence interpretations written this session show the grammar compresses well for teaching. But twelve declared choices stand between the statements and any single prediction, which puts CRR among the *expensive* frameworks for use, alongside the Free Energy Principle and causal-set growth dynamics, and far from Kolmogorov, Newton or special relativity, where the count is zero or one.
2. **The easement is real on the interpretive layer and absent on the predictive layer.** A reader can hold CRR's picture of time or of the origin of life after five sentences; that is a pedagogical virtue the record supports (the translation dossier found the glossary usable, the lenses were written without strain). A reader who wants to compute a cut or a bound must first make the twelve choices, calibrate σ on training data independent of the tested event, and declare the carrier, and the bottleneck runs showed how much the outcome depends on those choices (the noise-path problem in C1, C3, B3). That dependence is the price of the easement: the statements are short because the choices are deferred.
3. **The right comparison class is the framework theories, not the axiom systems.** Against Kolmogorov or ZFC, CRR looks over-parameterised; but those are axiomatisations of settled mathematics with nothing to predict. Against FEP, IIT, assembly theory and causal sets, CRR is of ordinary size in every column, with fewer named axioms than IIT, fewer pages than FEP, and more free choices than assembly theory. Whitehead's 45 categories are the reminder that a metaphysics can be stated at length without being usable; CRR's seven commitments are modest by that measure.
4. **What would improve the easement without weakening the claims:** fixing more of the twelve choices by convention for a *declared class of carriers* (as the core already does for the unit-covariance Gaussian: g = 1, ℓ = 2π), so that a student can compute an example with two choices rather than twelve. That is a documentation task, not a mathematical one, and it does not change any evidence status.
