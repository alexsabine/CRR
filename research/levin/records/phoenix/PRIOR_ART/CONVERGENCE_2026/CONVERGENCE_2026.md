# Independent 2026 convergence on CRR-like objects, and the CANDIDATE revisited (2026-10-06)

**Why this exists.** The owner, as a researcher, asked that cases where others in 2026 arrive at the same or similar constructions as CRR be highlighted rather than filed only as "redundant". Under the Egg this is legitimate on the prior-art layer: independent arrival at the same object is **evidence that the abstraction is natural**, which is a different thing from evidence that CRR is right or new. Every entry below was fetched in one of this session's R10 sweeps (`PRIOR_ART/CRR_TRANSLATION_2026-10-05/sweeps/`, `BOTTLENECK_CHALLENGES/2026-10-05_RUN/sweeps/`); versions are as seen there. The prior-art-auditor rubric (`AGENTS/roles/prior_art_auditor.md`) is applied: **same equation / same mechanism / same qualitative direction / similar language**. No entry is evidence for any CRR claim; none changes the ledger. None of these authors cites CRR, and there is no reason to think any was influenced by it.

## 2. The CANDIDATE revisited (bottleneck challenge B3, Nguyen et al. 2026)

**What was found.** Nguyen, Etzel, Bezdek and Zacks (eLife, 7 Jul 2026) report that error- and uncertainty-triggered boundary models leave "approximately 16% of the explainable variance" in human event-boundary judgments unaccounted for. The CRR reading suggested a third trigger type: a boundary when **accumulated change since the last boundary** crosses a threshold, which fires under slow drift that produces no error spike.

**What the computation showed.** In a toy with six abrupt jumps and six slow drifts, an error trigger caught 6 jumps and 1 drift; the CRR accumulated-*arc* trigger caught all 12 but with 29 false alarms, because on a noisy carrier the arc is dominated by noise path length; a standard displacement-since-last-boundary trigger (CUSUM-type) caught 4 jumps and 5 drifts with 4 false alarms. The grade was therefore REDUCES for the trigger *type* and WRONG for CRR's specific *object* (arc rather than displacement).

**What stands after revisiting.**

1. The **question** is live and belongs to CRR's reading: "is there a boundary mechanism driven by how much has changed, not by how surprising the change was?" None of the three models in the eLife paper has that form. That is a genuine candidate explanation for residual variance and the data are public (OpenNeuro ds005551).
2. The **detector** that would test it is the displacement (chord) rule, not the arc. In CRR's own vocabulary this is a *chord-triggered* cut, `C*` since the last boundary exceeding a threshold, which the core does not define (the core's cut is arc-based, on a circle). So the candidate is a proposal *suggested by* CRR and *answered by* standard change-point detection; if it works, the credit belongs to the question, and the result would be REDUCES as mathematics while still being new to the event-segmentation literature if no one has fitted it there. Prior art on cumulative-change boundary models in event cognition was not fetched in this session and must be before any claim.
3. **Registered next step (SEED, not run):** add a displacement-since-last-boundary regressor to the paper's FIR model on ds005551, preregistered threshold and smoothing, content-matched control (a random-interval regressor with the same event rate), outcome = share of the 16 % residual explained. This is a P12-style heuristic test: CRR supplies the hypothesis, the field supplies the instrument.

**Status:** CANDIDATE-as-question, REDUCES-as-detector. Recorded both ways so the downgrade in the bottleneck run is visible and the live part is not lost.

## 3. What a researcher may say in a report, and what not

May say, with citations above: that in 2026 at least six independent groups formalised objects identical to CRR's core quantities in their own domains, which supports describing CRR as a natural abstraction; that CRR's distinctive additions (the intrinsic circle, the antipodal cut, universal balance) are not among the converged objects; that the convergence suggests a shared grammar is useful for cross-reading these literatures, which is the translation-dossier verdict.

May not say: that CRR anticipated, predicted or explains any of these works; that convergence raises any CRR claim's evidence rung; that the CANDIDATE is a finding.
