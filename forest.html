A Living Forest

The same wood at this hour, on this day, where you are.

 
 
 

    What is known

    A tree's twigs grow towards free space and light. Runions, Lane and Prusinkiewicz (2007) made that into the space colonisation method. Leonardo noticed that the branches above a fork have together about the cross-section of the limb below. Shinozaki and others (1964) gave this a basis in their pipe model, and Eloy (2011) relates it to wind load.

    Where the sun and moon stand follows from the date, the hour and the place (Meeus 1998). Leaves are shed when a layer of cells at the foot of the stalk weakens (Addicott 1982). In real trees the timing follows warmth and day length (Körner and Basler 2010). Birds in a flock steer by their neighbours (Reynolds 1987).

    The figures are after L. S. Lowry (1887 to 1976), who painted people as small dark shapes on pale ground.

    The rule used here

    Each tree is grown once by space colonisation and thickened by the pipe rule (exponent 2.45, a setting). Leaf cover and colour follow the day of the year, moved by half a year south of the equator. The view looks towards the midday sun. The sun and moon are placed by calculation, and the light and the shadows follow them.

    The timing rule for small events is CRR: a count builds, and when it reaches a fixed threshold the event happens and the count starts again. A leaf lets go when its tree's count of wind reaches the threshold. A walker stops when a count of tiredness and meetings does. CRR is under investigation, and here it sets only the timing.

    Easter is computed (the anonymous Gregorian method in Meeus 1998), and so is Thanksgiving. The lunar feasts for 2026 and 2027 are listed from published interfaith calendars. Many move by a day with place or the sighting of the moon. In other years they are marked “about”.

    What is left out

    This is a picture, not a forecast. Real trees do not keep a calendar, and no species is modelled closely. The sky colours and the mist are painted, not computed from scattering. The moon may be a degree out.

    Without your leave the page does not ask where you are. It takes your longitude from your time zone, assumes latitude 52 north (or 35 south) and gives the wood typical weather for the season. “Local weather” asks your browser for your position and sends it, rounded, to open-meteo.com.

    On a festival day or its eve the wood carries one small mark. Where I was not sure how to mark a day with respect, it is a single lantern. To visit another day, add ?at=2027-04-10T08:00 to the address.
