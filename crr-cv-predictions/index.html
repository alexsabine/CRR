What CRR predicts about how regularly things repeat

CRR is a framework that predicts the coefficient of variation (CV) — the ratio of standard deviation to mean — of cycle times across very different systems, with no fitted parameters. A heart, a star, a switch, an atom: each gets a single predicted number from a single formula. This page explains exactly how, step by step, and lets you browse all 172 predictions in the package.

Why does CRR predict a CV at all?

CRR claims that every oscillating system — whatever it is — passes through the same three phases, in the same order, governed by the same equation. The coefficient of variation drops out of that structure as a number you can compute from the system's symmetry alone.

Phase 1

Coherence accumulates

The system gathers evidence about its own state. Anything that persists adds up. The integral C = ∫ L dτ measures how much has accumulated.

Phase 2

Rupture happens

When C·Ω = 1, the system reaches its information-theoretic limit (the Cramér–Rao bound) and reorganises. The transition is sharp: a heart valve snaps, a neuron spikes, an atom decays.

Phase 3

Regeneration rebuilds

What comes next is reconstructed from weighted memory: the higher the past coherence, the more accessible that memory is during rebuilding.

The single parameter Ω sets everything else: how much coherence the system can hold before rupture, how broadly it remembers, and — crucially for this page — how variable its cycle times are. Specifically, the CV of the time between ruptures is just Ω/2.

The single formula

Every prediction in this package comes from one formula. The only thing that changes from system to system is one number, φ_G — the geodesic length of the system's phase manifold.

CV = 1 / (2 · φ_G)

What is φ_G?

It's the length of the shortest closed loop on the system's phase manifold (the abstract space the system's state lives in), measured in the natural geometric units (the Fisher–Rao metric).

  • For a binary switch (Z₂ — two states like on/off, systole/diastole, fire/rest): the phase manifold is the Bernoulli manifold, with diameter π. So φ_G = π, and CV = 1/(2π) ≈ 0.159.
  • For a continuous cycle (SO(2) — anything that keeps rotating like a circle): the phase manifold is a circle of circumference 2π. So φ_G = 2π, and CV = 1/(4π) ≈ 0.080.
  • For a quantum spin-½ system (SU(2) — Bloch sphere): the geodesic is also 2π, so CV ≈ 0.080 (CRR predicts spin-½ has the same CV as a continuous cycle).

The formula is parameter-free: nothing has been fit to data.

Why divide by 2?

Because the rupture event is a Bernoulli draw — at the Cramér–Rao bound, the variance of an unbiased estimator equals 1/4 of the cycle. The CV picks up a factor of 1/2 from the conjugacy structure that links CRR to the Heisenberg uncertainty principle (where the same factor appears as the minimum uncertainty product).

Where does the 2 in the ratio come from?

For any binary system regulated by a continuous cycle: CV(Z₂) / CV(SO(2)) = (1/2π) / (1/4π) = 2, exactly. That's a topological invariant — it's the ratio of two manifold circumferences, not a fit. Any system where the observed ratio is 1.7 or 2.4 falsifies the framework.

The three readings of "Z₂"

The most subtle thing about CRR is that the symbol "Z₂" can mean three different things, and the predicted CV is different in each case. This is the single most important thing to understand about applying the rubric.

Reading 1 — pure

Z₂ only (memoryless)

CV = 1

A binary event with no continuous regulator anywhere in the causal chain. The exponential distribution. The atom's nucleus has no closed geometry to ride on, so the Z₂ baseline gets multiplied by the geodesic of the absent SO(2): (1/2π) × 2π = 1.

Examples: radioactive decay, single-photon arrivals from incoherent sources, M/M/1 queue inter-arrivals, spontaneous synaptic minis at low Ca²⁺.
Reading 2 — compositional (the default)

Z₂ on SO(2)

CV ≈ 0.159

A binary event with a continuous regulator somewhere underneath it. This is what almost every "Z₂" entry in the 132-system table actually is. The hinge is the SO(2); the door's open/closed is the Z₂ that rides on it.

Examples: heart valve (cardiac cycle behind it), neuron spike (membrane potential), light switch (rocker arc), volcanic eruption (chamber recharge cycle).
Reading 3 — observed directly

SO(2)

CV ≈ 0.080

A continuous cycle observed as a continuous cycle, not projected through a Z₂ event. The phase wraps from 0 to 2π with no preferred stopping point.

Examples: respiration during NREM sleep, segmentation clock, cardiac RR interval (in supine 5-min recording), ocean swell period.

Why it matters: the "every Z₂ has an SO(2) underneath" principle

On physical inspection, almost every Z₂ event humans encounter has a rotation somewhere in its causal chain. Every switch you have ever used travels an arc. The clock escapement is the principle made mechanical — a rotating wheel gates a binary tick.

The radioactive nucleus is the rare exception: the strong and EM forces cannot find a closed configuration, so there is no SO(2) substrate. The absence is quantified by the geometry of what is absent — a factor of 2π. The resulting CV is exactly 1.

The pre-registered classification protocol

This is the algorithm that was applied to every system in the 132-system table before any empirical CV was consulted. If you can answer the questions for a new system — using only its physics, never its observed CV — then the prediction is fixed.

  1. Is the system oscillatory?

    Does it have repeated temporal events with a measurable period or inter-event interval?

    If no, CRR does not apply — exclude. CRR is about cycle times; a once-off event has no CV.

  2. Identify the state space

    Ask in order; stop at the first "yes".

    QQuestionAssignn
    Q1 Two distinguishable states alternating? (on/off, fire/rest, inhale/exhale, contracted/relaxed) Z₂ 2
    Q2 Continuous cycle with no preferred stopping point? (a phase that wraps 0 → 2π without identifiable states) SO(2) 4
    Q3 n > 2 discrete phases per cycle? (4-stroke engine, cell cycle G1/S/G2/M, repressilator) Z_n n
    Q4 None of the above? (ambiguous structure) Z₂ (default) 2

  3. If you said Z₂, is there an SO(2) regulator in the causal chain?

    This step was added after Sabine's 2026 radioactive-decay finding. It refines the Z₂ classification.

    Has SO(2) regulator?Refined symmetryCVNotes
    Yes (default for any physical Z₂ system) Z₂ (= Z₂-on-SO(2)) 1/(2π) ≈ 0.159 What the 132-table assumes by default
    No — truly memoryless source Z₂_only 1 Radioactive decay, Poisson minis, M/M/1, etc.

    The default is Yes. Almost every physical Z₂ system has a continuous regulator underneath (the heart cycle behind the valve, the membrane potential behind the spike, the rocker arc behind the switch). Assert "No" only when memorylessness is established as a property of the source process.

  5. Three-class assignment (still no peeking at empirical CV)

    Classify on physics alone.

    • Class A — autonomous stochastic. No strong external regulation, no major noise injection. Intrinsic stochasticity dominates timing. CRR predicts the canonical CV within [0.6×, 1.3×].
    • Class B — deterministic / actively regulated. Feedback control, entrainment, engineering precision, or evolutionary synchronisation crushes Ω below the thermodynamic value. CRR predicts CV ≪ canonical (suppressed).
    • Class C — noise-dominated / volitional. External stochastic forcing, volitional/cortical modulation, low copy numbers, or point-process statistics inflate Ω above the thermodynamic value. CRR predicts CV ≫ canonical (elevated).

  6. Look up empirical CV. Compare. Document honestly.

    Find the observed CV in peer-reviewed literature. Compute ratio = cv_obs / cv_pred. Assign:

    RatioVerdictWhat it tells you
    0.60 — 1.30MATCH Timing variability consistent with the predicted symmetry class.
    < 0.60SUPPRESSED System is more precise than the prediction. Diagnostic: active regulation, feedback, engineering.
    > 1.30ELEVATED System is more variable than the prediction. Diagnostic: stochastic forcing, volitional modulation, asymmetric sub-cycles, or nested oscillation.

    A directional reversal — Class B predicted suppressed but actually elevated, or Class C predicted elevated but actually suppressed — is the strongest form of misprediction. The 132-system paper reports zero directional reversals.

Worked example: cardiac RR interval (paper row 1)

Step 1. Heart beats — yes, oscillatory.

Step 2. Q1 — is it two states? You could say systole/diastole, but in a 5-min supine recording the cardiac cycle is best treated as continuous phase progression. Q2 — continuous cycle? Yes. Assign SO(2), n = 4.

Step 2.5. Skipped — only applies to Z₂.

Step 3. CV_pred = 1/(4π) ≈ 0.0796. Acceptance band [0.0478, 0.1035].

Step 4. Resting supine, no entrainment — autonomous. Class A.

Step 5. Empirical (Nunan 2010 BJSM 44:202): CV = 0.054. Ratio 0.68× → MATCH. Class A predicted MATCH — no directional reversal.

Worked example: radioactive decay of Carbon-14

Step 1. Decay events — yes, oscillatory in the loose sense (repeated inter-event intervals).

Step 2. Q1 — two states? Atom intact / decayed. Z₂, n = 2.

Step 2.5. Is there an SO(2) regulator? No. The unstable nucleus has no closed geometry — strong and EM forces cannot find a balanced configuration. Reassign as Z₂_only.

Step 3. CV_pred = 1.0 (the exponential distribution).

Step 4. Pure point process — Class C.

Step 5. Empirical CV of inter-decay times = 1.0 exactly (theorem about the exponential distribution). MATCH.

    The asymmetry that proves it isn't numerology

    The most surprising prediction of the recent radioactive paper is that the exponential distribution's CV = 1 can be derived as CV(Z₂) × C*(SO(2)) = (1/2π) × 2π = 1 — the Z₂ baseline inflated by the geodesic of the absent SO(2) substrate. Two independent checks make this more than a coincidence.

    Z₂ × SO(2)

    (1/2π) × 2π = 1
    Equals unity ✓

    Removing the SO(2) regulator from a Z₂ event gives the memoryless Poisson statistics (CV = 1). Physically correct: the Z₂ requires an SO(2) for regulation.

    SO(2) × Z₂

    (1/4π) × π = 1/4
    Not unity ✗

    Removing a Z₂ projection from an SO(2) cycle does not produce memorylessness. Physically correct: a continuous rotation can exist without producing binary events.

    Discrete memoryless: no 2π factor

    The geometric distribution (the discrete analogue of the exponential) has CV = √(1−p)/p, which varies continuously with p. There is no clean 2π factor at any value of p. The 2π appears only on continuous manifolds — precisely where SO(2) could have existed but doesn't. On a discrete lattice there is no SO(2) to be missing.

    Class C systems approach the limit

    Several "elevated" Class C systems in the 132-table sit at high ratios that approach the theoretical maximum 2π = 6.28:

    • Spontaneous blink interval: 5.03×
    • Head direction cell ISI: 4.27×
    • Mast seeding interval: 3.77×
    • Inter-sigh interval: 3.77×
    • Earthquake recurrence: 3.14×

    These are systems whose SO(2) regulator is weak, intermittent, or absent over the observation timescale.

    Pre-registration discipline & falsification

    The credibility of CRR's predictions depends on never letting empirical CV values back-feed into the classification. The protocol is designed to make that discipline auditable.

    What's pinned in tests

    The package's test suite (pytest tests/) pins:

    • 132 paper rows; 53 MATCH / 34 SUPPRESSED / 45 ELEVATED.
    • Class totals A 45, B 40, C 47 → 114/132 = 86% three-class correct.
    • Zero directional reversals.
    • CV(Z₂) × C*(SO(2)) = 1 exactly; reverse = 1/4 (asymmetry).
    • Topological CV-equivalences hold across symmetry families.
    • Monte Carlo: exponential distribution CV → 1 (as a theorem).

    What would falsify the framework

    • A system whose Z₂/SO(2) ratio is 1.7 or 2.4 (not 2).
    • A directional reversal in a clean Class B or Class C system.
    • Two CV-equivalent groups (e.g. SU(2) vs SO(2)) showing different observed CVs in well-prepared experiments.
    • A demonstrably memoryless physical system with CV ≠ 1.
    • A demonstrably regulated Z₂ system with CV ≈ 1.

    What this package does not claim

    • It does not fit any parameter — every CV prediction is computed from the canonical formula CV = 1/(2·φ_G).
    • It does not adjudicate the open theoretical questions in the paper's Section 8 (constant-speed geodesic traversal, circular Landauer barrier, mean-interval-as-closed-geodesic identification). It records them.
    • It does not replace the 24+ independent claim notebooks in claims/. It is a predictions catalogue that points at them.
    • It does not include re-runs of the paper's Monte Carlo null-model significance — those live in crr-engine/consistency/.
